Westmark State Cosmetology Act, amendment 7 (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-003).

Hair braiding (braids, twists and locs, with no cutting or chemicals) was previously unregulated in Westmark. About 2,000 people braid for pay, mostly self-employed, many working from home or in small shops. Two neighboring states do not license braiding; their borders are a long drive from the main cities.

Anyone who braids hair for pay, and any business that employs them, must hold a full cosmetology license: 1,500 hours at a licensed cosmetology school (typical tuition $12,000 to $18,000) and a state exam. The curriculum covers cutting, coloring and chemical treatments; braiding is a small part or absent. There are no exemptions. Existing braiders have 12 months to comply.

The State Board of Cosmetology enforces the requirement. Fines apply for unlicensed practice and for businesses that employ unlicensed braiders. Inspections are complaint-driven.

Unclear provision: whether unpaid braiding for family and friends is covered. Assumed not covered (only braiding for pay is).
