Kestrany Sugar Import Regime Act (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-047).

Kestrany consumes about 1,750,000 tonnes (t) of sugar a year, about 60% by food and drink manufacturers and 40% by households. Domestic beet sugar supplies about 1,100,000 t, from about 4,000 growers on about 120,000 ha and two processors (Ardmouth Sugar and the Pellmont Beet Co-operative) with six mills running at about 90% of capacity. Imports averaged about 650,000 t a year in 2023 to 2025: about 450,000 t of raw cane sugar refined at two port refineries in Vellport, and about 200,000 t of white sugar. Kestrany takes about 1.2% of world sugar trade, bought from many exporters. World prices (illustrative): raw $360 a tonne, white $440. Under the existing flat 25% ad valorem tariff, white sugar lands at about $550 a tonne, which sets the domestic white price; tariff revenue is about $62.5 million a year. Nine firms imported in 2023 to 2025: the two Vellport refiners (66% of imports), four trading houses (25%), and three manufacturers that import directly (a soft-drink bottler and two confectioners, 9%). Kestrany's food manufacturers export about a fifth of their output, and imported confectionery, biscuits and soft-drink concentrates (not covered by the sugar tariff) hold about a quarter of the home market. Two wet mills make glucose syrup from domestic maize. Kestrany's main exports are timber, machinery and tourism.

The bill, including the base period, was published on 2 March 2026, passed on 10 June 2026, and takes effect for the quota year beginning 1 January 2027 (ten months after it was proposed). No expiry; the quota volume is reviewed in 2031.

Jurisdiction: Kestrany's customs territory. Outside: the world sugar market, and foreign makers of sugar-containing goods, which the act does not cover. Customs applies the rates; the Ministry of Trade's licence office keeps the register of quota shares and licences. Imports above a firm's licence pay the out-of-quota rate; misdeclaration is penalized as under existing customs law. The licence office's funding is not stated.

Two-rate tariff (K1), replacing the flat 25%. Importers of record pay a tariff of 2% ad valorem on imports of raw and white sugar covered by a licence, up to 400,000 t a year in total, and 60% ad valorem on all other sugar imports. A licence covers raw or white sugar tonne for tonne. Sugar-containing goods are not covered (as now).

Allocation by history (K2). Each firm that imported sugar in 2023 to 2025 receives a permanent share of the 400,000 t equal to its share of total imports in those years. Each December, licences for the next year are issued to share holders pro rata.

Trading (K3). Shares and licences may be sold or leased to any registered importer; transfers are registered with the licence office for a $200 fee. Registration as an importer is open to any firm for $500.

Surrender (K4). Section 9: "A licence not used by 30 September of the quota year is surrendered."

Existing rules that stay in the counterfactual: the flat 25% tariff on raw and white sugar; customs classification of sugar mixtures; the general budget, which receives the tariff.

Unclear provision: surrendered licences. Section 9 surrenders licences not used by 30 September. Section 4(2): "The tariff quota shall be allocated in full in each quota year." Section 12: "The Minister may make rules for the allocation of quantities among registered importers on application." Reading U1-a: surrendered quantities return to the quota and must be allocated again in the same year (section 4(2)), to registered importers on application under section 12. Reading U1-b: surrender cancels the licence; section 4(2) refers to the December issue, and the surrendered quantity lapses for that year. The ministry has not said which it will apply.

At the old landed price of about $550 a tonne (white), Kestrany imports about 650,000 t, 250,000 t more than the quota. Without the act, imports would continue at about 650,000 t, spread evenly through the year (about 160,000 t a quarter).
