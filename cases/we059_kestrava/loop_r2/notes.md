## Where the format could not hold what the policy says

1. **A duty to supply that opens a closed trade.** K1 makes makers sell parts, tools and software to independents and owners on the authorized-provider terms, where before they refused (A-2). A `held below` on that exchange would sign a prevented-trade fall (CR-5a) when the trade rises from near zero; a `charge` on the inputs would also fall. So K1 is a mandated term on the sale of the equipment (as the policy words it), and the independents are reached only by links from the owners' receipt. The price line in "fair and reasonable terms" (the authorized providers' price) is not a fact of the engine.
2. **No binding or enforcement premise on a charge.** `branch_premises` (meant for a payer's choice) carries a2 (binds) and a1 (most comply); A-10 (the Attorney General acts on complaint only) fits as an alternative (legal_breach) but not as a weaker enforcement of the term.
3. **One receiver per charge.** K1 is owed to "the owner and to any independent repair provider"; the charge names `growers` or `owners.phone`. The independents' side of K1 is a link, not a receipt.
4. **A quantity premise carries one direction.** A-25 (costs outweigh gains) supports a fall in sales; A-22 (buyers value access to repair inputs) pushes the other way and cannot be a premise on the same fact. It does not appear.
5. **A void term is not a ban.** K2 voids a term; the sale goes on. The seed uses a ban on invented exchanges (sales on restrictive terms, sales with pairing locks), so the signs are a fall in trade on those terms; `term_prevalence`, the variable that names the effect, cannot be signed by the engine. K3 is the same, under U1-a only (U1-b lists no component).
6. **A class that is receiver and payer.** Growers receive the K1 term (a "better" chooser, uses alternatives less) and pay a higher list price (a "worse" chooser), so their alternative and their timing come out `ambiguous` (repairs; dealers' quantity). Dealers' quantity needed a dominance premise, which settles the reported sign but does not carry down a link (the sign stays two chains downstream).
7. **A reading's sign in a table row.** A-24 holds two claims (integration, and pairing more parts under U1-b); a premise is one statement, so the same text sits under a plain alternative and one with `reading: U1-b`, with no separate sign. Likewise A-17 and A-15 name the reading in their wording.
8. **Enforcement on complaint.** `fixed_budget` gives enforcement_cost one decrease and the enforcer one increase, so `officials.enforcement` is `ambiguous`/both-signed; the format has no "acts only on complaint", and no complaints variable.
9. **One price nationwide.** Phones sell at one price in every state; the format cannot say that a Kestrava rule moves no Kestrava phone price, or that a program is extended nationally (A-8 is an alternative that raises out-of-state owners' receipts). The farm list price (A-18) is a `moves` fact on the buyer side only.
10. **Parts of a class and a size line.** The 14 specialty models with fewer than 20 sales (A-11) are a threshold by model volume; `thresholds` takes an actor and a measure, not a model count, so A-11 is a makers' alternative (growers buy across the line). The program maker (already compliant) is expressed only by leaving it out of `manufacturers.phone_other`; its K2 and K3 position sits in `manufacturers.phone`.
11. **Used equipment and phone resale, theft victims.** A-15 and the stolen-phone chain stop at owners' asset value and an informal-activity variable for sellers of stolen parts; there is no variable for thefts or victims.
12. **Dealers' functions.** `retailers.dealers` stands for dealers' equipment sales, parts and service; the policy and A-4 treat the repair capacity separately from sales.
