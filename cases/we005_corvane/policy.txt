Corvane Emergency Fuel Price Order (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-005).

Corvane is a state of about six million people. On Day 0 a fire shuts the Port Aldis refinery, which makes about 40% of the gasoline sold in the state. The owner expects repairs to take 8 to 12 weeks. Corvane requires a special clean-burning blend that few refineries outside the state make; replacement cargoes come by tanker and take 2 to 4 weeks to arrive. A products pipeline carries gasoline from Corvane refineries east to the neighboring state of Brennick, which accepts Corvane's blend. Its capacity is about a tenth of Corvane's consumption. The eastern Corvane cities are 20 to 45 minutes' drive from the Brennick line.

Before the outage, regular gasoline averaged $3.90 a gallon in Corvane. By Day 3 it was $5.10. In Brennick it was about $4.00. On Day 3 the governor announced that a cap would take effect on Day 5. The order expires on Day 95. Wholesale (rack) prices are not capped.

Regular gasoline sold at retail in Corvane is capped at $4.20 a gallon including taxes, midgrade at $4.50 and premium at $4.80, from Day 5 to Day 95. Diesel is exempt, and so are deliveries to commercial fleets under contracts signed before Day 0.

No fees, surcharges or required purchases may be tied to a fuel sale; the posted price must be the pump price.

The State Attorney General enforces the order. Fines are up to $5,000 per violation, inspectors check posted prices, and there is a public hotline. Enforcement is funded by an emergency appropriation from the general fund.

The order is silent on whether stations may limit the quantity sold per customer, shorten their opening hours or refuse card payments.
