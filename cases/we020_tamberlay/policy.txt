Tamberlay Home Start Grant (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-020).

Tamberlay is a state of about 3 million people. About 60,000 homes are sold there in a normal year, roughly a third of them for $450,000 or less, and first-time buyers account for about 18,000 purchases. Builders complete about 12,000 new homes a year; zoning approval and permits take 12 to 24 months and construction another 9 to 15, so the stock of homes responds slowly to prices. Tamberlay's largest city, Kessford, sits on the border with the state of Lowen, whose suburbs are within a 30-minute commute and which has no comparable grant. Entry-level home prices in Tamberlay have been rising about 4% a year.

The governor announced the grant in the budget speech on 10 March of Year 1. The legislature enacted it on 15 May. It applies to purchase contracts signed from 1 July of Year 1 to 30 June of Year 4, with a legislative review in Year 3. Renewal is possible but not promised.

The grant is $15,000 for first-time buyers who apply, paid at settlement and usable toward the deposit, for the purchase of a home in Tamberlay. The contract price must be $450,000 or less (fixed in money terms). The home must be the buyer's principal residence for 24 months; otherwise the full grant is repaid.

Neither the applicant nor the applicant's spouse or declared partner has ever owned residential property, anywhere. Ownership in Tamberlay is checked against the land register; ownership elsewhere and partner status are by sworn declaration. One grant per household.

The grant and its administration are funded by an annual appropriation from general revenue, about $300 million a year (20,000 grants). The law does not say what happens if applications exceed the appropriation.

The State Revenue Office checks the land register and declarations, and checks occupancy against driver's license and electoral roll addresses. Clawback and fines apply for false declarations.

Unclear provisions: whether the price cap will be indexed if prices rise (the text fixes it in dollars); whether the grant will be renewed after Year 4.
