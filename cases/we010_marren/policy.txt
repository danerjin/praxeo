Marren Tobacco Tax Act (FICTIONAL: Marren, Dunmarch, Kessick and the Act are invented for handbook worked example WE-010 and resemble, but are not, any real state cigarette tax).

Marren is a state of about 6 million people. Its capital, Port Alder, lies on the river that forms the border with Dunmarch; three bridges cross it. About 45% of Marren's population lives within a 20-minute drive of Dunmarch or of Kessick, its southern neighbor. Per-pack cigarette taxes are $1.50 in Marren, $0.40 in Dunmarch and $0.80 in Kessick; Dunmarch has not changed its rate in 15 years. Before the act, a typical pack cost about $7.50 in Marren and $5.80 in Dunmarch.

The bill was introduced in June, passage was expected from early August, it was signed on 1 October, and it took effect on 1 January.

Licensed stamping agents (wholesalers), who buy tax stamps and affix them before sale to retailers, must pay a cigarette tax raised from $1.50 to $3.50 per pack of 20, fixed in money terms (not indexed). Other tobacco products (roll-your-own tobacco, cigars, "little cigars") stay at 25% of the wholesale price. E-cigarettes are untaxed.

A floor-stock tax of $2.00 per stamped pack applies to packs held by wholesalers and retailers at 00:01 on 1 January; packs held by consumers are not taxed.

One carton (10 packs) per person every 30 days may be brought in from other states without Marren tax; anything more owes Marren use tax, self-reported. Possession of more than 5 cartons of unstamped cigarettes is a misdemeanor; more than 50 cartons is a felony with seizure of the vehicle. The Revenue Department's Tobacco Unit grows from 12 to 20 agents, with road checks near bridges, retailer audits and counterfeit-stamp detection.

Of the revenue from the $2.00 increase, $0.30 per pack goes to the Tobacco Unit and to the existing quit-smoking program (already funded from general revenue); the rest goes to the general fund.

It is unclear whether the use tax on cartons above the allowance will be collected at all in practice.
