Brisklund Wheat Price Assurance Act (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-065).

Brisklund is a country of about 9 million people with its own currency, the thaler (Th). About 18,000 farms grow wheat on about 570,000 hectares; at about 7.0 tonnes a hectare they produce about 4.0 million tonnes a year. About 2.5 million tonnes are used at home (1.4 by millers, 1.1 as animal feed) and about 1.5 million tonnes are exported. World wheat production is about 800 million tonnes and world trade about 200 million; Brisklund's exports are under 1% of world trade. The farm-gate price follows the export price less freight. Over the past ten marketing years the national average farm-gate price ranged from Th 175 to Th 285 a tonne, averaged about Th 232, and was below Th 220 in four of the ten years, by about Th 20 on average in those years. Milling varieties fetch about Th 18 a tonne more than feed varieties and yield about 0.6 tonnes a hectare less. Barley (about 1.2 million tonnes, part exported) competes with wheat for land and with feed wheat in animal feed. About 40% of wheat land is rented, on leases of three to five years; wheat land sells for about Th 12,000 a hectare and rents for about Th 300 a hectare a year. Neighbors grow wheat without such payments; grain crosses the borders by rail and road.

The Farm Register, kept by the Ministry of Agriculture, records for each registered farm its annual harvest declarations and, since 2026, its "established production": the average of its wheat output in the crop years 2022 to 2026, entered for a crop-insurance scheme that has since ended. Registration requires that agricultural activity is carried out on the farm. About 3.8 million tonnes of established production are recorded in all.

The bill was introduced with the government's majority on 10 February 2027 and enacted on 30 June 2027. It covers the crop years 2028 to 2032 (the harvests of those years; winter wheat for 2028 is sown in autumn 2027). Parliament must review the target price before 2032.

A deficiency payment is paid to the holder (the person registered as farming the land) of each registered farm. The payment rate is Th 220 less the national average farm-gate price for the marketing year, when that price is below Th 220; otherwise zero. It is paid in December after the marketing year. On the past ten years' prices, the expected payment is about Th 7 to Th 8 a tonne a year, and in a year at Th 180 the payment is Th 40 a tonne: about Th 160 million on 4.0 million tonnes or Th 152 million on 3.8 million tonnes of established production, against a general budget of about Th 40 billion. Futures for the 2028 harvest trade at about Th 228. A grower switching a hectare from a milling to a feed variety gains about 0.6 tonnes and loses Th 18 a tonne on 6.6 tonnes: at a payment of Th 8 a tonne the switch gains about Th 5 a hectare more than without the payment; at Th 40, about Th 24.

The payments are funded as follows: "Such sums as are needed for payments under this Act shall be paid out of the Consolidated Fund." The Ministry of Agriculture's payments office administers the claims on the Farm Register, at a cost of about Th 4 million a year from its general appropriation. Harvest declarations are checked by sampling (about 3% of farms a year); false declarations are subject to repayment and a fine of up to twice the payment. The Farm Register and its registration rule stay as they are; there is no tariff or quota on wheat, and leases are freely negotiated.

Unclear provision. Section 4(1) of the Act says: "A deficiency payment is payable to the holder of each registered farm on the farm's wheat production for the crop year." Section 2 says: "'Wheat production', in relation to a farm, means the production of wheat recorded for the farm in the Farm Register." The Register records both annual harvest declarations and established production (the 2022 to 2026 average). On one reading the payment is on the harvest declared for the crop year (actual output). On the other it is on the farm's established production, "for the crop year" fixing only the year whose price sets the rate. The Ministry has issued no guidance.
