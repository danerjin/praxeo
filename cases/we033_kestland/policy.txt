Kestland Household Support Payment Act 2027 (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-033).

Kestland is a country of 9.6 million people. Its address register lists 4.0 million households; the Tax Agency has 4.6 million tax-filing units with an adult member. The economy produces about 420 billion crowns a year. Government debt is about 45% of that, and the government sells about 35 billion crowns of bonds in a typical year; about 30% of its bonds are held abroad. About 35% of households report less than one month's income in liquid savings. A survey of intentions by Kestland Statistics in March 2027 found that 30% of households planned to save most of the payment, 40% to pay bills or debts, and 30% to spend it on goods and services. A 2014 energy rebate of 300 crowns per household was followed by higher card spending in the following two months.

The Finance Minister announced the payment in the budget speech on 4 March 2027. Parliament enacted it on 29 April 2027.

Payment. Households resident in Kestland on 1 April 2027 receive 1,000 crowns per household, once, tax-free, not counted as income for benefits. The Tax Agency pays by direct deposit from 1 July 2027 to the 3.4 million households with bank details on file. Others apply by form, with identity documents, by 31 December 2027, and are paid within eight weeks. There are no exemptions. Non-resident citizens are not eligible.

Borrowing. The Treasury sells ten-year "Support Bonds", up to 4.1 billion crowns, at auction from May to August 2027 to banks, pension funds, other private buyers and foreign buyers. The bonds cover the payments and up to 60 million crowns of administration.

Repayment. The principal and interest on the bonds are repaid from general revenue in ten equal annual installments from 2028. No tax is named. The Finance Ministry says repayment will come within existing tax rates, through spending restraint. The opposition says taxes will rise.

Administration and enforcement. The Tax Agency runs payment runs, application processing, a call center and identity checks. Bank details on file can be changed online with a password. False claims must be repaid, with a fine of 2,000 crowns, and may be prosecuted as fraud. The administration is funded from the borrowing.

Unclear provision. The Act defines a household as persons living together at one address and sharing living expenses. The Tax Agency's first guidance pays by tax-filing unit. Under one reading there is one payment per registered address. Under another reading there is one payment per tax-filing unit, so an address with an adult child or a lodger who files separately receives two. Under the second reading about 4.6 million payments are due, more than the 4.1 billion crowns of borrowing covers.
