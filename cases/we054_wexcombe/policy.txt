Wexcombe Airport Night Curfew and Slot Order (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-054).

Wexcombe International Airport (WXI) is run by the Wexcombe Airport Authority (WAA), a public authority owned by the City of Wexcombe and two counties, which sets the airport's operating rules and charges. In the summer 2025 season WXI handled about 420 movements (a takeoff or a landing) a day: about 380 between 06:00 and 23:00, and about 40 between 23:00 and 06:00. Of the night movements, about 22 are an express parcel carrier's night sort (the "integrator"), about 6 are two other cargo carriers, and about 12 are passenger flights (8 late arrivals scheduled 23:00 to 00:30 and 4 early departures scheduled 05:15 to 05:55). On top of these, about 6 movements a night are daytime flights scheduled before 23:00 that run late and operate between 23:00 and 00:00; about 1 a night operates after midnight. The runway's declared capacity is 34 movements an hour (an existing rule, set by the WAA from air traffic control's assessment); the peaks 07:00 to 09:00 and 17:00 to 20:00 run at 30 to 34, the hour 06:00 to 07:00 at about 10 and the hour 22:00 to 23:00 at about 12. Daytime movements have grown about 3% a year and night cargo about 5%. Fourteen airlines operate in the daytime; the three largest operate about 60% of daytime movements. Aircraft average about 150 seats; airlines can move to larger aircraft only as fleets turn over (two to four years), and three of WXI's gates take wide-bodied aircraft.

WXI employs about 11,000 people on site, including about 900 (mostly part-time night shifts) in the integrator's sort; about 30% of airport workers live within 8 km. About 9,000 homes lie inside the WAA's night-noise contour (the area where night noise exceeds the WAA's threshold), with a median value of about $310,000. The WAA's charges (about $2,000 per movement on average, about $13 per seat) are set each year to recover its costs, which are mostly fixed. Its noise office, which handles residents' complaints, costs about $0.5 million a year; about 60% of its cases concern night movements. The WAA estimates the slot coordination unit and curfew monitoring at about $2.8 million a year.

Pellham Regional Airport, 70 km away by motorway (about 55 minutes), is run by a separate authority, has no curfew, and uses about 45% of its runway capacity. About 1,200 homes lie in the area that would be inside a night-noise contour at Pellham if night cargo moved there; Pellham's commuting area holds about 40,000 homes. Overnight parcel delivery is sold in a national market; Wexcombe's region is about 4% of it.

The WAA board published the draft order, including the base seasons, on 2 March 2026, after a residents' campaign for a curfew that had run since 2023. It adopted the order on 15 September 2026. Slots are issued on 1 September 2027; the curfew and the cap take effect with the winter season on 31 October 2027. No expiry; the WAA will review the cap in 2032.

This Order applies to aircraft movements at Wexcombe International Airport. Enforcement is by the WAA, from air traffic control's movement log. The penalties are $50,000 per movement in the curfew without a dispensation and $10,000 per daytime movement without a slot; slots of a holder fined three times in a season may be withdrawn. Enforcement is funded from airport charges.

Curfew (K1), a prohibition of takeoffs and landings from 23:00 to 06:00. It binds all operators at WXI, with no movements permitted, subject to U1. Emergencies, medical and state flights are exempt.

Daytime cap (K2), a quota. Movements from 06:00 to 23:00 are capped at 380 a day (the summer 2025 scheduled number), binding all operators at WXI; the existing hourly limit of 34 stays. The exemptions are as for K1.

Allocation by history (K3), a rights allocation. The 380 daily slots are issued free to airlines in proportion to the daytime movements each operated in the summer 2026 and winter 2026/27 seasons, each slot in the hour of the movement that earned it. The WAA issues them.

Trading (K4). Slots may be sold or leased, and retimed to another daytime hour where the hourly limit leaves room. This binds slot holders.

Freed slots (K5), a rights allocation. Slots of a holder that ceases to operate at WXI, or that uses a slot on fewer than 80% of a season's days, return to the WAA and are reissued free, first to night operators (those with night movements in winter 2026/27) in proportion to their night movements, then to new entrants.

Enforcement and funding (K6). The penalties are as above; the WAA recovers its costs through the annual charges.

Existing rules, in force in the counterfactual too: the hourly limit of 34 movements; the WAA's cost-recovery charge method.

Unclear provision U1, late-running flights. K1 says: "No aircraft shall take off or land between 23:00 and 06:00, save that a flight scheduled to operate before 23:00 and delayed by circumstances beyond the operator's control may be permitted to operate by the duty manager." The WAA's explanatory note says late-running flights "may operate until midnight"; the order itself names no time and no criteria. Reading U1-a: a flight scheduled before 23:00 and delayed may land or take off until 00:00, as of right. Reading U1-b: there is no dispensation for ordinary delays; after 23:00 only emergencies operate, and a late flight diverts (in practice to Pellham) or is cancelled.

The counterfactual is WXI without the order: no curfew, no cap, no slots, daytime and night movements growing as before, and the residents' campaign continuing.
