Corrin Lirva Control Act (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-017).

Lirva is the dried leaf of the lirva shrub, brewed as tea or chewed for a mild stimulant and relaxing effect. It has been sold legally in the State of Corrin (about 6 million people) for fifteen years, in about 400 specialist tea shops, in convenience stores and online, and has been subject to the general sales tax. About 180,000 adults use it regularly. A typical dose is about 5 g of leaf. A concentrated extract, made by soaking the leaf in alcohol and drying the result, gives the same effect at about 0.25 g; it is about 5% of current sales. The neighboring state of Halvet keeps lirva legal; its border is 40 to 90 minutes' drive from Corrin's two largest cities. The Act was passed six months before it took effect.

From the effective date, making, selling, possessing for sale, importing and growing lirva, its active compound lirvine, and any product containing either are prohibited, except for licensed research.

Penalties are graded by the total weight of material, including any carrier or mixture. Possession under 50 g is a civil fine of $250; 50 g to 1 kg is a misdemeanor, up to 1 year; over 1 kg is a felony, 2 to 10 years. Sale is punished one grade higher than possession of the same weight.

Existing stocks must be destroyed or exported by the effective date. No compensation is paid.

The state police drug unit, local police and the state laboratory enforce the Act, with an added budget for investigators and lab testing from the general budget.

Unclear provision: whether related plants with similar compounds are covered. The Act names only lirva and lirvine, so they are assumed not covered.
