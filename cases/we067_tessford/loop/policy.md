## 1. Policy record

**Tessford Tenant Stability Ordinance 2027 (Ordinance 27-14).** Tessford is a city of about 310,000 people (128,000 households, 81,000 of them renters) in Ambry County (about 640,000 people). The rest of the county is the town of Corvell (92,000 people, 12 km away, about 14,000 rental homes), the town of Wick Hollow (36,000 people, 25 km), and unincorporated areas: about 41,000 rental homes outside Tessford, with a vacancy rate of about 4%. Across the county line, 18 km east, is Durnside (Selcombe County; 60,000 people, about 11,000 rental homes, no rent regulation), 22 minutes from central Tessford by rail. No rent regulation exists anywhere in the region today.

**The market.** Tessford's market rents rose about 6% a year in 2021 to 2026; the regional consumer price index rose about 3% a year. Covered buildings (below) hold 52,000 rental homes, almost all occupied (vacancy 3%); the median rent in them is about $2,000 a month. Exempt rental homes: about 17,000 flats in buildings first occupied in or after 1990, and about 12,000 rented houses and condominiums. About 55% of covered tenancies are month to month; the rest are 12-month leases. State law lets a landlord end a month-to-month tenancy without cause on 60 days' notice; in 2026 there were about 1,100 such terminations in buildings that would be covered.

**Who moves** (the city's 2026 mover survey and tenancy records). About 18% of covered households move out each year (about 9,400). Of those who move, about 38% leave Tessford (about 3,550 a year): 60% of them to elsewhere in Ambry County, 40% beyond it. 31% of those who left Tessford gave a rent increase as the main reason. Households with a member aged 65 or over: 7,800 in covered homes, 6,400 of them with at least three years in their present home. They move out at about 6% a year (about 470); 45% of senior movers leave Tessford (about 210 a year): about 115 to elsewhere in the county and about 95 beyond it, of whom about 35 go to Durnside. Non-senior households leave Tessford at about 3,340 a year. Of households displaced by building sales in 2019 to 2024 (relocated under the state's assistance program), 42% left Tessford and 17% left Ambry County.

**Figures for the arithmetic.** Cap: 60% of the price index, about 1.8% a year. If market rents keep rising 6% a year, a $2,000 covered rent compares with the market as follows: year 3, $2,110 against $2,382 (gap $272, 11%); year 5, $2,187 against $2,676 (gap $489, 18%); year 10, $2,391 against $3,582 (gap $1,191, 33%). The planning department projects that 150 to 400 units a year will be withdrawn under §9 from the second year (condominium prices per unit are 30% to 40% above the value per unit of a capped building); about 15% of households in withdrawn buildings would be seniors, as in the stock. The City's budget office estimates the senior credit (§7) at $0.6 million in 2028, rising to about $9 million by 2032, from a general fund of about $1.1 billion; no tax is named. The rent board's fee (§11) raises about $4.7 million a year.

**The ordinance.** In November 2026 a majority of the newly elected council and the mayor campaigned on rent regulation (a 2022 petition for a ballot measure had failed to qualify). The ordinance was introduced on 1 March 2027, adopted on 11 May 2027 and takes effect on 1 October 2027. Council review after five years (2032); no expiry.
- §1 (purpose): "to protect Tessford's tenants from displacement and to allow long-standing residents, especially older residents, to remain in their homes and in their community."
- §2 (covered units): "every rental dwelling unit in Tessford in a building of two or more units first occupied before 1 January 1990." Single houses and condominiums rented individually, and buildings first occupied on or after that date, are exempt.
- §3 (cap): "The rent of a sitting tenant may rise by no more than 60 per cent of the annual change in the regional consumer price index in any twelve months, from the base rent." The base rent is the rent in force on 1 October 2027, or for a later tenancy the initial rent. "On a new tenancy the landlord may set the initial rent."
- §4 (tenancy): eviction only for cause (non-payment, breach, nuisance, owner move-in, withdrawal under §9). A tenancy may not be assigned; a sublet needs the landlord's consent and may not be at more than the tenant's rent.
- §7 (Portable Senior Credit): "(a) A tenant aged 65 or over who vacates a covered unit after at least three years' continuous tenancy in it, on or after the effective date, may apply for a Portable Senior Credit. (b) The credit is the amount by which the registered market rent of the vacated unit exceeds the rent the tenant would have paid for it under §3, recomputed on each anniversary of vacating. (c) The City pays the credit monthly to the landlord of any rental dwelling in Ambry County in which the tenant resides, up to the rent of that dwelling. (d) The credit ends when the tenant ceases to reside in Ambry County, or dies." The registered market rent is set each year by the rent board from the rents of comparable exempt units in the same neighbourhood.
- §9 (withdrawal): "(a) An owner may withdraw all the units in a building from the rental market on 120 days' written notice, or one year's notice to a tenant aged 65 or over or disabled, (b) paying each household a relocation payment of $8,000, or $12,000 for a household with a member aged 65 or over or disabled. (c) The relocation payment is in full settlement of the tenant's claims under this Ordinance. (d) A withdrawn unit may not be offered for rent within five years, and if offered later, only at the rent that would have applied under §3." Owners usually sell withdrawn units as condominiums (state law allows conversion with a subdivision map) or occupy them.
- §11 (rent board): registration of covered units; complaint-driven enforcement; fines up to $5,000 per violation, with refunds of overcharges; funded by an annual fee of $90 per covered unit paid by the landlord and not recoverable from tenants. The board also sets the registered market rent schedule.
- §12 (administration of the credit): under an interlocal agreement, the Ambry County Housing Office pays the credit to landlords outside Tessford, for a fee of 4% of the payments, paid by the City.

Existing rules that stay in the counterfactual: state landlord-tenant law (60 days' notice to end a month-to-month tenancy without cause; the county court hears eviction cases); the state's relocation assistance program for building sales; state condominium conversion law.

Unclear provision:
- **U1, displaced seniors and the credit.** §7(a) gives the credit to a qualifying senior who "vacates a covered unit". §9(c) says the relocation payment "is in full settlement of the tenant's claims under this Ordinance". Reading U1-a: a senior who leaves on a withdrawal notice "vacates" the unit, and the credit is a payment by the City, not a claim against the owner, so §9(c) does not bar it; displaced seniors receive both the $12,000 and the credit. Reading U1-b: §9(c) settles all claims "under this Ordinance", §7 included; displaced seniors receive the $12,000 only. The board has issued no guidance and has no practice yet. U1 bears on the roughly 15% of households in withdrawn buildings that are seniors with three years' tenure.

Readings get no probability; where a sign depends on U1, one sign per reading is given.

Not analyzed: how the board will compute the registered market rent (treated as a premise on the size of the gap, A-1, not as a reading); whether an owner can withdraw some units of a building and not others (the text says "all the units").
