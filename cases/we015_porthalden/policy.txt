Port Halden Taxi Fleet Stabilization Ordinance (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-015).

Port Halden is a city of about 900,000 people. Until now, anyone who passed a driver background check and a vehicle inspection could obtain a city taxi license for $400 a year; about 2,400 licenses are in use. Demand for taxi trips has grown about 3% a year with population and tourism. Fares are set by the city council as a meter rate, indexed each year to consumer prices; this rule predates the ordinance and is unchanged by it. The suburbs of Brindlewick and Corvale license their own taxis, which may drop passengers in Port Halden but may not pick up there. The ordinance was introduced by the council five months before it took effect, with passage widely expected.

The number of taxi licenses is fixed at the number outstanding on the effective date, and applies to anyone offering taxi service: street hails and taxi ranks. New licenses are issued only by council vote. Licenses become "medallions" that may be sold or leased, with transfers registered and a 2% transfer fee. Driver and vehicle standards are unchanged; vehicles must be no more than 8 years old.

Vehicles that take only bookings made at least 30 minutes ahead, at a fare agreed at booking, may operate with an uncapped private-hire permit ($300 a year). There are no street hails and no ranks for private hire.

The Taxi and Limousine Office enforces the ordinance with 12 inspectors. The fine is $1,000 for unlicensed street-hail or rank service, and the vehicle is impounded on a second offense. Enforcement is funded from the general budget and license fees.

Unclear provision: whether a booking made through a smartphone app a few minutes before pickup meets the 30-minute rule. The rule was written for phone bookings to dispatch offices, and the office has not ruled. Reading U1-a: app booking counts as pre-booked. Reading U1-b: it does not.
