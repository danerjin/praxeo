Calvar Sugar Market Act (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-014).

The Republic of Calvar (about 20 million people) uses about 1.2 million tonnes of sugar a year. Domestic farmers grow sugar beet, which four beet factories turn into about 450,000 tonnes of white sugar. The rest, about 750,000 tonnes, is imported, mostly as raw cane sugar that two refineries at the ports of Quellport and Varo refine. World sugar prices have been below the cost of most Calvarian beet growers for years. Corn syrup is made from domestic corn by two wet mills. Calvar's only land neighbor, Ostria, has no sugar quota; the border town of Varnholt (150,000 people) is 20 minutes from Ostrian supermarkets, but Calvar's main cities are 200 km or more from the border. The bill was introduced with government backing nine months before the effective date and passed four months later.

Imports of raw and refined sugar, and of mixtures containing more than 65% sugar by weight, are limited to 350,000 tonnes a year from the effective date, and importers are bound by the limit. The quota has no expiry, but parliament must review it after five years. Sugar contained in finished foods, mixtures with 65% sugar or less, and a travelers' personal allowance of 10 kg per crossing are exempt. Corn syrup and other sweeteners are not covered.

The annual quota is divided into shares that are allocated free to importers, in proportion to each importer's sugar imports over the 36 months before the effective date. Shares are permanent and may be sold or leased; transfers are registered with customs.

The customs service enforces the quota. Imports without quota rights are seized and fined three times their value. Bulk cargo may enter only through Quellport and Varo, where every shipment is inspected; the land crossing at Varnholt is checked by sample. Enforcement is funded from the general budget.

Unclear provision: whether the 65% threshold counts sugar against dry weight or total weight. Assumed total weight, the reading more favorable to importers of syrups and blends.
