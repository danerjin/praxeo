San Francisco Proposition I (November 1994): extension of rent control to small multi-unit buildings.

NOTE: This is a short summary written for the thin-slice test, not the legal text of the measure. It is to be replaced by the text of the ordinance and the ballot pamphlet before the case counts in any evaluation.

Since 1979 the San Francisco Residential Rent Stabilization and Arbitration Ordinance has limited annual rent increases for sitting tenants in most rental buildings built before June 1979. Buildings of four units or fewer in which the owner lives were exempt.

Proposition I removes that exemption. Rental units in owner-occupied buildings of two to four units built before June 1979 become subject to the Rent Ordinance.

For a covered unit, the landlord may raise the rent of a sitting tenant once a year by no more than the allowable increase, which is 60 percent of the rise in the regional consumer price index.

When a tenant leaves voluntarily, the landlord may set the rent for the next tenant freely, after which the limit applies again.

Tenants of covered units may be evicted only for one of the just causes listed in the Rent Ordinance.

The Rent Board hears tenant petitions and landlord applications and may order rent reductions and refunds.

The measure was approved by the voters on November 8, 1994, and takes effect for covered units from the date of its certification.
