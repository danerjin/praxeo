Halvern Steel Safeguard Order (FICTIONAL: Halvern and the order are invented for handbook worked example WE-013 and resemble, but are not, any real safeguard measure).

Halvern is a mid-sized trading country with its own currency and a floating exchange rate. Three domestic steel firms supply about 60% of the steel used in Halvern; the rest is imported. About 4,000 domestic manufacturers use steel as a main input (machinery, vehicle parts, household appliances, cans, construction products); machinery makers export about 35% of their output. Halvern's other large export industries are grain and tourism. Halvern buys a small share of the world's steel.

The Trade Ministry opened an investigation in September, published its recommendation on 1 January, and the order took effect on 1 April.

Importers of record must pay a tariff of 25% of the customs value on imports of flat-rolled and long steel products, for three years. A review in the third year may extend it. Imported fabricated steel products and finished goods containing steel (machinery, vehicles, appliances, fabricated structural sections, fasteners) are not covered.

Steel users may apply to exclude a product "not produced domestically in sufficient quantity or quality". Domestic steelmakers may object. Decisions are due within 90 days; exclusions last one year and must be renewed. The Customs Service checks classification at ports and penalizes misdeclaration. The revenue goes to the general budget.

The standard for "sufficient quantity or quality" is not defined.
