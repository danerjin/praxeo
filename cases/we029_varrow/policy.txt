Varrow Healthy Workplaces Act (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-029).

The State of Varrow has about 6.2 million people and 2.7 million private-sector employees. Its largest city, Fenmouth, lies on the river that forms the border with the State of Pellam, which has no paid sick leave law; about 40,000 people commute across the river each day. Varrow's minimum wage is $13.50 an hour, indexed each year to consumer prices; about 9% of private employees earn at or near it. A survey by the state labor department found that about 62% of private employees already receive paid sick leave, or paid time off (PTO) that can be used for illness, and about 38% (about 1.0 million) do not. The uncovered are concentrated in food service, retail, personal care, agriculture and construction, and about 55% of them work part time. About 9% of private employees are covered by collective agreements that fix wages for one to three years.

The bill was introduced on 12 January 2026 with the governor's support, passed on 28 April 2026, signed on 15 May 2026, and takes effect on 1 January 2027. It has no expiry.

Every employee accrues one hour of paid sick time for every 30 hours worked, from the first hour of work or from 1 January 2027 for existing employees, up to 40 hours accrued in a calendar year. Paid sick time is paid at the employee's regular hourly rate. Independent contractors, as defined by the state's existing classification test, and public employers are not covered.

Sick time may be used for the employee's own illness, injury or medical care, or a family member's. It is usable after 90 days of employment (existing employees: from 1 April 2027), in increments of one hour. Notice is due "as soon as practicable". The employer may require documentation only for absences of more than three consecutive scheduled workdays, may not require the employee to find a replacement, and may not count sick time as an absence under an attendance policy.

Unused accrued hours carry over to the next calendar year. The employer may cap the balance at 80 hours and use at 40 hours a year. Unused hours are not paid out at separation; an employee rehired within six months gets the balance back. The carry-over rule is enforced from 1 January 2028.

An employer whose policy provides at least 40 hours a year of paid time usable for the same purposes on the same conditions is deemed compliant.

Employers with fewer than six employees are exempt. Size is the "average number of employees during the previous calendar year".

The Department of Labor Standards enforces the Act on complaint only, within two years of the violation; there are no routine audits. Remedies are back pay for sick time denied or unpaid, plus an equal sum as damages to the employee, and a civil penalty up to $1,000 for retaliation. Employers must post a notice and keep records for three years. A new appropriation of $3.4 million a year from the general fund pays for 25 investigators.

The existing minimum wage of $13.50 an hour and the existing test that classifies workers as employees or independent contractors stay in force.
