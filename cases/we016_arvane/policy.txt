Arvane Interior Design Practice Act (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-016).

Interior design was previously unregulated in the State of Arvane: anyone could offer it for pay. About 6,000 people do, roughly 40% of whom hold a degree in interior design. About half the trade's revenue comes from commercial work (offices, shops, restaurants), half from homes. Two accredited degree programs in Arvane graduate about 300 students a year. Arvane's largest city, Corvan, is 20 km from the border with Velmark, which does not license interior designers. The Act was passed ten months before it took effect.

Anyone who offers interior design for pay in Arvane, and any firm that employs them, must hold a license. Interior design means space planning, and selection of interior finishes and furnishings, for homes or businesses in Arvane. A license requires a four-year accredited degree in interior design, 3,500 hours of work supervised by a licensed designer or architect, and a state exam ($1,200). The license fee is $250 a year, with 12 hours of continuing education a year. Architects, retail employees advising on goods sold by their employer, work on one's own home, and unpaid work are exempt.

Anyone who documents three years of paid practice in Arvane before the effective date and registers within 18 months receives a license without the degree, hours or exam. The license lapses if not renewed, and cannot then be regained without meeting the full requirements.

Plans for building permits for commercial interior alterations must be stamped by a licensed interior designer or an architect. Previously, non-structural interior plans by anyone were accepted. Homes are exempt from this rule.

The State Board of Interior Design enforces these rules. Enforcement is complaint-driven, with fines up to $2,500 per violation, and contracts for unlicensed practice cannot be enforced in court. The Board is funded by license fees and the general budget.

Unclear provision: whether choosing furnishings, finishes and colors without any space planning ("styling") is covered. Read as covered (the definition lists "selection of interior finishes and furnishings") or as not covered (only space planning, or styling done with it, is covered).
