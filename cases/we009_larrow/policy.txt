Larrow Fair Wage Act (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-009).

Larrow is a state of about five million people, with a minimum wage of $10 an hour. Wages for low-paid work have been rising about 3% a year. About 15% of Larrow workers earn under $12 and about 22% under $14. Workers under 20 are about 5% of the workforce but about 20% of those earning under $12; most work in restaurants, retail and recreation. The neighboring state of Dorran keeps a $10 minimum. The border cities of Larrow Falls (Larrow) and Port Dorran face each other across a river, ten minutes apart by three bridges.

The bill was introduced in December of Year 0 and passed in March of Year 1. It takes effect in two steps, on January 1 of Year 2 and Year 3. It is not indexed after the second step.

The adult minimum wage for employees aged 20 and over, paid by all employers in Larrow, is $12 an hour from January 1, Year 2 and $14 an hour from January 1, Year 3. The existing exemptions remain (family members on family farms; certain apprentices). There is no tip credit, before or after.

Employers may pay employees under 20 at least 85% of the adult minimum: $10.20 in Year 2 and $11.90 in Year 3.

An employer may not dismiss an employee aged 20 or over, or cut their hours, in order to replace them with a worker paid the youth rate.

The State Labor Department enforces the Act through complaint-driven investigations, with about 40 investigators; remedies are back pay plus an equal amount in damages, and workers may sue. Enforcement is funded from the general budget.
