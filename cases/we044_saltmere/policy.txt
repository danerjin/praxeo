Saltmere Housing Preservation Ordinance (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-044).

Saltmere is a coastal city of about 310,000 people with about 128,000 dwellings, of which about 60,000 are rented long-term (median rent $1,850 a month, vacancy about 3%). Tourism is concentrated in three districts near the beach (Harbor, Dunes and Old Town). The city's 2026 platform report counts about 4,300 active short-term rental (STR) listings: about 1,250 are rooms in a home where the owner lives and is present; about 2,150 are whole units that are not the owner's primary residence (about 1,700 never used by the owner, and about 450 second homes the owner uses some weeks a year, with about 70% in the three beach districts); about 550 are whole units that are the owner's primary residence, let while the owner is away (about 140 let more than 90 nights a year); and about 350 are whole units in two-unit buildings whose owner lives in the other unit (about 120 let more than 90 nights a year).

A whole-unit STR earns on average $235 a night at 60% occupancy, about $51,000 a year gross; after cleaning, platform fees, furnishing and management (about 40% of gross), about $30,000 net. The same unit let on a 12-month lease earns about $24,000 gross, about $20,000 net. Hotels have about 7,400 rooms, average occupancy 79% (94% in July and August) and an average daily rate of $180; hotel construction takes three to five years from site to opening. Furnished lets of 30 nights or more (traveling nurses, university visitors, relocations) are a small segment of about 900 units, rented at about $3,200 a month. About 6% of a year's whole-unit STR nights are booked more than nine months ahead. In 2023 to 2025, about 30% of home purchases in the beach districts were by buyers who did not occupy the home. Outside the city, the town of Elsdon, 12 km from the Harbor district, has about 420 STR listings and no rules beyond county registration.

The ordinance applies to every dwelling unit within the City. It was introduced on 3 November 2026 by six of nine council members, enacted on 8 December 2026, and takes effect on 1 July 2027, eight months after introduction, with a transition for booked stays to 30 September 2027. It has no expiry.

From 1 July 2027, no whole unit may be let for under 30 nights unless it is the owner's primary residence, registered under the registry, and then for no more than 90 nights in a calendar year (45 nights for July to December 2027). Primary residence means the dwelling in which the owner resides at least 270 days a year. Stays booked and confirmed before 1 July 2027 may be completed if they end by 30 September 2027; all other bookings for later dates must be cancelled and refunded in full. Rooms in a home that is the owner's primary residence, while the owner is present, and lets of 30 nights or more are exempt.

From 1 July 2027, booking platforms must display a valid registration number on every whole-unit listing, remove a listing within 10 days of a city notice that it is non-compliant, and report nights booked per registration number each quarter. The fine is $1,000 per listing per day after the 10 days.

Owners who let their primary residence must register for $250 a year, with proof of residence (driver's license, voter registration, homestead tax exemption). The registry is run by a new Registry Unit of five staff at $520,000 a year, a new appropriation from the general fund. It replaces the existing STR registration for whole units.

Code Enforcement enforces the ban against hosts on complaint only, with no proactive inspection and no new appropriation. The fine is $2,500 for a first violation and $5,000 for each later one, within existing appropriations, and fines go to the general fund. Code Enforcement has a fixed budget of $6.1 million (48 staff).

The existing rules stay in force without the ordinance. STR registration costs $150 a year, and about 65% of hosts are registered; platforms are not obliged to check. A transient occupancy tax of 12% applies to stays under 30 nights, hotels and STRs alike, and platforms collect it for STRs (about $18.7 million a year from whole-unit STRs and about $46 million from hotels). A noise and nuisance ordinance is enforced by Code Enforcement on complaint; in 2025 there were about 9,600 noise complaints, about 1,500 of them about STR units.

Unclear provision: whether the primary-residence exception covers the second unit of a two-unit building whose owner lives in the other unit. The exception covers "the dwelling in which the owner resides". Reading U1-a: the dwelling is the building, so the second unit is part of the primary residence and may be let up to 90 nights a year. Reading U1-b: the dwelling is the unit, so the second unit may not be let for under 30 nights at all. About 350 listings are affected; about 120 of them let more than 90 nights a year. The Registry Unit will decide which reading it applies when it issues or refuses registration numbers.
