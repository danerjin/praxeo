Aldenmoor Home Inspector Licensing Act (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-055).

The State of Aldenmoor has about 68,000 home sales a year. About 54,000 buyers commission a pre-purchase inspection; sellers commission about 4,000 pre-listing inspections, and builders and buyers about 3,000 inspections of new homes under construction: about 61,000 inspections a year. The average fee is about $450. About 610 people inspect homes for pay. About 400 began practice before 1 January 2025 (they will have three years of practice by 31 December 2027); of these, about 320 will have at least 250 fee-paid inspections in the three years before that date at their present pace, about 60 began practice between 1 July 2024 and 31 December 2024 and reach three years only during the application window, and about 80 are part-timers with three years or more but a pace of 40 to 80 inspections a year. About 210 began on or after 1 January 2025 and cannot reach three years by 31 December 2027. About 70 people enter a year and about 50 leave. About 45% of inspectors hold a voluntary national certification (100 hours of training and an exam); about 40% carry no errors-and-omissions or liability insurance. About 35 licensed professional engineers inspect homes. A state survey of agents finds that about 70% of buyers hire an inspector their agent recommended.

Approved training will cost about $3,200 in tuition and about three weeks of time; the exam about $200, with an expected pass rate of about 70%; the required insurance ($300,000 of errors-and-omissions and general liability cover) about $1,800 a year; the license fee $250 a year. The neighboring State of Linmouth has no license; about 12% of Aldenmoor's sales are in the Brask River metro on the border, where about 40 Linmouth inspectors do some Aldenmoor jobs.

The bill was introduced on 12 January 2026 and enacted on 3 June 2026. Training courses are approved from 1 January 2027 and the exam is offered from March 2027. From 1 July 2027 no one may begin practice without a license; those in practice on that date may continue until 31 December 2027; from 1 January 2028 every inspector must hold a license. No expiry.

Inspections of residential property in Aldenmoor are covered, wherever the inspector lives. Linmouth, across the Brask River, has no license.

K1, license. Anyone inspecting a home for pay in Aldenmoor must hold a license: 120 hours of approved training, a state exam, $300,000 of insurance and $250 a year. A license not renewed for 12 months can be restored only by meeting the requirements for a new license. Licensed professional engineers and architects are exempt.

K2, grandfathering. Incumbent inspectors are licensed without training or exam on application between 1 July and 31 December 2027, if at the date of application the applicant has at least three years of practice and at least 250 fee-paid inspections in those three years, documented by reports. Insurance and fee are still required.

K3, enforcement on complaint. The Home Inspector Board, with two investigators, is funded by license, exam and application fees. Unlicensed practice carries a fine of up to $2,500 per inspection; licensees face suspension or revocation. The Board shall investigate an alleged violation of this Act only upon a written complaint by a buyer of residential property or by a licensed real-estate agent.

K4, agents and inspectors. A real-estate licensee shall not direct, steer or require a client to use a particular home inspector.

Unclear provision U1, agents' recommendations. K4 forbids an agent to "direct, steer or require" a client to use a particular inspector. Reading U1-a: the provision forbids only requiring or conditioning (making a transaction depend on the inspector); an agent may still name or recommend inspectors when asked, as now. Reading U1-b: naming a particular inspector is "steering"; an agent may give a client only the Board's public list of licensed inspectors.

About 210 incumbents (34%) cannot be grandfathered and must train or stop. The counterfactual is Aldenmoor without the Act: no license, inspectors entering and leaving as now, agents recommending inspectors as now, and the inspectors' association continuing the campaign for licensing that it had run since 2022.
