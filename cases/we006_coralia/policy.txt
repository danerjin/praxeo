Coralia Emergency Pricing Act (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-006).

Coralia is a coastal state. The Act was passed two years ago and is a standing law: it applies automatically whenever the governor declares a state of emergency for named counties. This season a hurricane is forecast to strike the six coastal counties (about 1.8 million people). The governor declares an emergency for those counties two days before landfall. A declaration lasts 30 days and may be renewed in 30-day steps; the governor has said it will be renewed "as long as needed". The inland counties of Coralia, 60 to 150 miles from the coast, are not declared. The neighboring state of Hallorn, about three hours north, has no such law. For the first two or three days after landfall, roads into the coast are partly blocked.

The price of generators and fuel cans, bottled water, plywood, tarps and other building materials, and hotel, motel and short-term rental rooms sold or rented in the declared counties may not exceed 110% of the seller's own average price in the 30 days before the declaration. A seller who did not sell the item then may not charge more than 110% of the average price of the item in that county before the declaration. The rule binds every seller of these goods in the declared counties, including sellers from outside the area selling there and online sellers delivering there, for the whole declared emergency. There are no exemptions.

A seller may add any documented increase in its own costs (purchase price, freight) since the base period, but may not raise its markup.

The State Attorney General enforces the Act. The civil penalty is up to $10,000 per violation; repeat violations are a misdemeanor. There is a public hotline, staff are deployed to the declared area, and cases are publicized. Enforcement is funded from the Attorney General's budget.

The list names goods and lodging only; whether repair and tree-removal services are covered, and whether minimum-stay rules and deposits count as part of a room's price, is unclear.
