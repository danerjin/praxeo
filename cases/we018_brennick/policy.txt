Brennick Carryout Bag Ordinance (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-018).

Brennick is a city of about 400,000 people. Retailers there have given away thin plastic carryout bags at the checkout, at a cost to them of about 2 cents a bag, built into their prices. About 120 million such bags are handed out a year. Surveys suggest that about 60% of households reuse some of them as bin liners or for pet waste. The neighboring town of Fallow Creek has no bag rules; two large supermarkets there are within 1 km of the city line on Route 9, close to several Brennick neighborhoods. Brennick's other edges are mostly residential or rural. The ordinance was passed nine months before it took effect.

From the effective date, all retailers in Brennick are prohibited from providing single-use plastic carryout bags (plastic bags with handles, thinner than 4 mil (0.1 mm)) at the point of sale. Exempt are bags without handles for produce, meat and bulk goods inside the store, pharmacy prescription bags, and restaurant takeout bags.

Recycled-paper carryout bags may be provided only for a charge of at least 10 cents a bag, kept by the retailer and shown on the receipt. Reusable bags (designed for at least 125 uses; at least 4 mil if plastic) may be sold at any price. Customers paying with food-assistance benefits receive paper bags free.

The City Office of Sustainability enforces the ordinance on a complaint-driven basis: a written warning, then fines of $100, $250 and $500 per day of violation. Enforcement is funded from the general budget.

Unclear provision: how a bag's design for 125 uses is verified. The ordinance sets no test, so a maker's claim is assumed to be accepted.
