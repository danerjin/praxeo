Tarnesse 2.9 GHz Award Regulations (fictional; summary of the policy record of handbook worked example WE-064).

Tarnesse is a country of about 12 million people with its own currency, the mark (M). Three mobile network operators serve it: Ardel Mobile (about 42% of subscriptions), Nevra Telecom (36%) and Kostel (22%). Each holds national licenses in three existing bands (about 110, 100 and 70 MHz in total respectively), all expiring in 2035. Mobile data traffic grows about 30% a year, and busy-hour capacity in the cities is tight; without new spectrum, operators add capacity by adding cell sites in their existing bands ("densification"). Fixed broadband: Linmark Fibre passes about 25% of homes with fibre; Pellow Networks' copper (DSL) lines serve about 1.6 million households, including about 700,000 outside fibre and cable areas. There are about 11,000 tower and rooftop sites, most owned by two tower companies (Selwyn Towers and Haldmoor Sites); new towers need municipal permits that take 4 to 9 months. About 1,400 certified tower riggers do about 3,000 site installations a year. All radio equipment is imported; Tarnesse buys about 0.4% of the world's mobile radio equipment.

The 2,880 to 3,000 MHz band ("the 2.9 GHz band", 120 MHz) has been unused since a defence radar left it in 2024. Under the Radio Act, no one may transmit on spectrum without a license. The Tarnesse Communications Authority (TCA) consulted on releasing the band in March 2026; the Award Regulations were made on 1 March 2027. The auction ran in September and October 2027; licenses run from 1 January 2028 to 31 December 2042. Five bidders qualified: the three operators, Linmark Fibre (which plans fixed wireless access outside its fibre footprint) and Ombra Group (a foreign operator seeking entry). A financial investor registered interest but did not qualify by the deposit date under the TCA's provisional reading of regulation 9(3).

Figures. Six blocks of 20 MHz at a reserve price of M40 million each (M240 million in all). The TCA's consultant valued the blocks at M100 million to M200 million each; projected receipts are M600 million to M1.2 billion, against a general budget of about M58 billion a year and annual borrowing of about M2 billion. Covering 90% of the population with this band takes about 6,500 sites per network; covering the cities and large towns (about 65% of the population), which operators said in consultation they would cover commercially within five years, takes about 3,000. Three to four networks building to 6,500 sites each is 20,000 to 26,000 installations in five years, 4,000 to 5,000 a year, beside the existing 3,000. About 2,000 new towers are projected. The TCA's added costs: M9 million for the auction and M2 million a year for monitoring coverage, funded by an increase in its annual appropriation from the general budget.

Jurisdiction. Tarnesse, nationally. Neighboring countries license the same band in their own ways; cross-border coordination limits power near borders. The Regulations were announced and made on 1 March 2027, the auction ran in September and October 2027, licenses take effect on 1 January 2028, the coverage deadline is 31 December 2032 and licenses expire on 31 December 2042. The TCA enforces: coverage is verified by drive tests and operators' coverage maps; a licensee that has not met the coverage obligation by the deadline has its license revoked, without refund.

Auction (K1). The Regulations create six national licenses of 20 MHz in the 2.9 GHz band, sold to bidders who qualify by deposit. It is an ascending clock auction with a reserve of M40 million a block. The term is 15 years, with no right of renewal ("the Authority may re-auction or renew at market value").

Build-out obligation (K2). Each licensee must provide service in the band to areas containing 90% of the population by 31 December 2032, or the license is revoked without refund.

Cap (K3). No more than two blocks may be awarded to, or held by, one person and its affiliates. Affiliates are those where one controls more than 50%, or has the right to appoint a majority of the board. The cap binds each bidder and later holder.

Receipts (K4). "Payments under these Regulations shall be paid into the Consolidated Fund." The Treasury receives the auction payments.

Administration (K5). The TCA runs the auction and monitors coverage; its added costs are M9 million for the auction and M2 million a year for monitoring, funded by an increase in its annual appropriation from the general budget.

Existing rules that stay in the counterfactual: the Radio Act (no transmission without a license); the operators' existing licenses, expiring 2035; tower permits; no regulation of mobile or broadband prices.

Unclear provision. Whether a license may be resold before the coverage deadline. Regulation 14: "A licence may be transferred, in whole or in part, with the consent of the Authority, and the obligations in regulation 9 pass with it." Regulation 9(3): "Until the coverage obligation is met, the licence is held by the licensee for the purpose of meeting it." Reading U1-a: transfers are allowed at any time with consent (reg. 14), and reg. 9(3) only states the purpose of the obligation. Reading U1-b: reg. 9(3) bars any transfer until the obligation is met or the deadline passes. The TCA has issued no guidance; its provisional reading for the deposit date is not a decision on the Regulations.

The counterfactual is Tarnesse without the Award Regulations: the band stays unused, as the Radio Act requires for spectrum that is not licensed, and the question of whether and how to release it stays open.
