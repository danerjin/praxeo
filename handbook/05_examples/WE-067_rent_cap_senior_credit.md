# WE-067 Rent cap on sitting tenancies, with a withdrawal route and a senior credit portable within the county

Analyst: draft (Handbook 1.3, first use of PC-21)  |  Rule catalog version: 1.3  |  Status: awaiting expert review

A stylized, fictional policy in a fictional city and county. It is not modeled on any real ordinance and is not drawn from the historical case PC-21 came from. It is the first independent use of PC-21 (insiders leave the area less), and it is designed to exercise every part of that pattern: its If and Then (a binding cap whose benefit is tied to a sitting tenancy in Tessford); its subclass split (a withdrawal route, and an announcement window in which tenancies can still be ended without cause, displace some insiders); its population caveat (seekers shut out of Tessford settle elsewhere in the county and across the county line); and its Unless clause, tested with a benefit that is portable over **part** of the region only: a credit that follows tenants aged 65 or over to any rental home in Ambry County, and is lost if they leave the county. The counterfactual throughout is **Tessford without the ordinance**: the same city, county, state landlord-tenant law and rent trend, with no cap, no credit and no rent board. The probabilities are illustrative; the evidence column says what a real analysis would cite.

## 1. Policy record

**Tessford Tenant Stability Ordinance 2027 (Ordinance 27-14).** Tessford is a city of about 310,000 people (128,000 households, 81,000 of them renters) in Ambry County (about 640,000 people). The rest of the county is the town of Corvell (92,000 people, 12 km away, about 14,000 rental homes), the town of Wick Hollow (36,000 people, 25 km), and unincorporated areas: about 41,000 rental homes outside Tessford, with a vacancy rate of about 4%. Across the county line, 18 km east, is Durnside (Selcombe County; 60,000 people, about 11,000 rental homes, no rent regulation), 22 minutes from central Tessford by rail. No rent regulation exists anywhere in the region today.

**The market.** Tessford's market rents rose about 6% a year in 2021 to 2026; the regional consumer price index rose about 3% a year. Covered buildings (below) hold 52,000 rental homes, almost all occupied (vacancy 3%); the median rent in them is about $2,000 a month. Exempt rental homes: about 17,000 flats in buildings first occupied in or after 1990, and about 12,000 rented houses and condominiums. About 55% of covered tenancies are month to month; the rest are 12-month leases. State law lets a landlord end a month-to-month tenancy without cause on 60 days' notice; in 2026 there were about 1,100 such terminations in buildings that would be covered.

**Who moves** (the city's 2026 mover survey and tenancy records). About 18% of covered households move out each year (about 9,400). Of those who move, about 38% leave Tessford (about 3,550 a year): 60% of them to elsewhere in Ambry County, 40% beyond it. 31% of those who left Tessford gave a rent increase as the main reason. Households with a member aged 65 or over: 7,800 in covered homes, 6,400 of them with at least three years in their present home. They move out at about 6% a year (about 470); 45% of senior movers leave Tessford (about 210 a year): about 115 to elsewhere in the county and about 95 beyond it, of whom about 35 go to Durnside. Non-senior households leave Tessford at about 3,340 a year. Of households displaced by building sales in 2019 to 2024 (relocated under the state's assistance program), 42% left Tessford and 17% left Ambry County.

**Figures for the arithmetic.** Cap: 60% of the price index, about 1.8% a year. If market rents keep rising 6% a year, a $2,000 covered rent compares with the market as follows: year 3, $2,110 against $2,382 (gap $272, 11%); year 5, $2,187 against $2,676 (gap $489, 18%); year 10, $2,391 against $3,582 (gap $1,191, 33%). The planning department projects that 150 to 400 units a year will be withdrawn under §9 from the second year (condominium prices per unit are 30% to 40% above the value per unit of a capped building); about 15% of households in withdrawn buildings would be seniors, as in the stock. The City's budget office estimates the senior credit (§7) at $0.6 million in 2028, rising to about $9 million by 2032, from a general fund of about $1.1 billion; no tax is named. The rent board's fee (§11) raises about $4.7 million a year.

**The ordinance.** In November 2026 a majority of the newly elected council and the mayor campaigned on rent regulation (a 2022 petition for a ballot measure had failed to qualify). The ordinance was introduced on 1 March 2027, adopted on 11 May 2027 and takes effect on 1 October 2027. Council review after five years (2032); no expiry.
- §1 (purpose): "to protect Tessford's tenants from displacement and to allow long-standing residents, especially older residents, to remain in their homes and in their community."
- §2 (covered units): "every rental dwelling unit in Tessford in a building of two or more units first occupied before 1 January 1990." Single houses and condominiums rented individually, and buildings first occupied on or after that date, are exempt.
- §3 (cap): "The rent of a sitting tenant may rise by no more than 60 per cent of the annual change in the regional consumer price index in any twelve months, from the base rent." The base rent is the rent in force on 1 October 2027, or for a later tenancy the initial rent. "On a new tenancy the landlord may set the initial rent."
- §4 (tenancy): eviction only for cause (non-payment, breach, nuisance, owner move-in, withdrawal under §9). A tenancy may not be assigned; a sublet needs the landlord's consent and may not be at more than the tenant's rent.
- §7 (Portable Senior Credit): "(a) A tenant aged 65 or over who vacates a covered unit after at least three years' continuous tenancy in it, on or after the effective date, may apply for a Portable Senior Credit. (b) The credit is the amount by which the registered market rent of the vacated unit exceeds the rent the tenant would have paid for it under §3, recomputed on each anniversary of vacating. (c) The City pays the credit monthly to the landlord of any rental dwelling in Ambry County in which the tenant resides, up to the rent of that dwelling. (d) The credit ends when the tenant ceases to reside in Ambry County, or dies." The registered market rent is set each year by the rent board from the rents of comparable exempt units in the same neighbourhood.
- §9 (withdrawal): "(a) An owner may withdraw all the units in a building from the rental market on 120 days' written notice, or one year's notice to a tenant aged 65 or over or disabled, (b) paying each household a relocation payment of $8,000, or $12,000 for a household with a member aged 65 or over or disabled. (c) The relocation payment is in full settlement of the tenant's claims under this Ordinance. (d) A withdrawn unit may not be offered for rent within five years, and if offered later, only at the rent that would have applied under §3." Owners usually sell withdrawn units as condominiums (state law allows conversion with a subdivision map) or occupy them.
- §11 (rent board): registration of covered units; complaint-driven enforcement; fines up to $5,000 per violation, with refunds of overcharges; funded by an annual fee of $90 per covered unit paid by the landlord and not recoverable from tenants. The board also sets the registered market rent schedule.
- §12 (administration of the credit): under an interlocal agreement, the Ambry County Housing Office pays the credit to landlords outside Tessford, for a fee of 4% of the payments, paid by the City.

Existing rules that stay in the counterfactual: state landlord-tenant law (60 days' notice to end a month-to-month tenancy without cause; the county court hears eviction cases); the state's relocation assistance program for building sales; state condominium conversion law.

Unclear provision:
- **U1, displaced seniors and the credit.** §7(a) gives the credit to a qualifying senior who "vacates a covered unit". §9(c) says the relocation payment "is in full settlement of the tenant's claims under this Ordinance". Reading U1-a: a senior who leaves on a withdrawal notice "vacates" the unit, and the credit is a payment by the City, not a claim against the owner, so §9(c) does not bar it; displaced seniors receive both the $12,000 and the credit. Reading U1-b: §9(c) settles all claims "under this Ordinance", §7 included; displaced seniors receive the $12,000 only. The board has issued no guidance and has no practice yet. U1 bears on the roughly 15% of households in withdrawn buildings that are seniors with three years' tenure.

Readings get no probability; where a sign depends on U1, one sign per reading is given.

Not analyzed: how the board will compute the registered market rent (treated as a premise on the size of the gap, A-1, not as a reading); whether an owner can withdraw some units of a building and not others (the text says "all the units").

**The counterfactual law (CR-1, Recording).** Without the ordinance, landlords may end month-to-month tenancies without cause and set any rent at renewal; tenants pay the market rent wherever they live; no credit exists. Against that law, K1 attaches a payment (the gap) to the option "stay in this covered unit", for each sitting tenant. K4 attaches the same payment, for a qualifying senior, to every option "rent a home in Ambry County" after leaving. So for a qualifying senior the operations are: stay, improved by the gap; move anywhere in the county, improved by the same gap; move beyond the county, unchanged. Within the county set, the two operations improve every option equally: by CR-4 ("a change in means alone"), that is a change in means for whoever stays in the set, and it changes no relative cost within the set. Across the county line it changes relative cost: every county option is improved against every option beyond. This is what the analysis has to do with PC-21's Unless clause (section 5; Gaps (e)).

**Non-qualifying insiders (C-8, C-9).** PC-21 applied as written. For a tenant who will not qualify, the gap accrues only while they keep the unit: K1 improves "stay" and no other option, so by CR-4 (x: leave Tessford, y: stay) those for whom leaving was ranked narrowly above staying switch to staying. PC-21's second route ("fewer of them are priced out") is the same move: a tenant priced out was one for whom leaving ranked above staying at the market rent, and the gap is paid only to those who stay. One premise is missing from the pattern. Rent is a large share of these households' means, so CR-4's exception applies: the gap also raises their means, and savings built from it can make a move affordable later (a purchase in Corvell or Durnside). The decrease needs a premise that the forfeit of the future gap outweighs this (A-27; CR-12e, empirical premise). The same premise is needed for PC-7's turnover sign (C-8).

**Qualifying seniors: where PC-21's Unless clause is tested (C-10, C-11, C-52).** PC-21's If requires that "the protected buyers would not get an equal benefit at a destination outside the area". For qualifying seniors that is true of destinations beyond Ambry County and false of destinations elsewhere in it, where §7(b) pays exactly the gap they would have kept by staying, recomputed each year. The derivation is therefore done per destination set (section 2, counterfactual law):
- *Beyond the county* (C-10). Every county option, staying included, is improved by the gap; options beyond are not. By CR-4 some seniors who would have left the county switch to a county option: some stay in their unit, some move elsewhere in the county. Moves out of the county decrease. The CR-4 exception (savings that later fund a move beyond the county) is settled by the same premise as for other insiders (A-27). PC-21's sign holds, for this variable.
- *Out of Tessford* (C-11). Two chains. (−) Those in C-10 who switch to staying in their unit leave Tessford less; those who switch to elsewhere in the county still leave Tessford, so they do not change it. (Means) Between staying and moving elsewhere in the county, the policy changes no relative cost: both options carry the same gap. For a senior who stays in the county the gap is a change in means alone, and which county option the added means go to depends on the senior's ranking (CR-4). Some who would have been priced out of Tessford to cheaper Corvell may now stay; some who would have stayed in Tessford at the market rent may now afford a move to Corvell near family. That chain is itself `ambiguous` (kind 3, CR-12 0.8), so the combined sign is `ambiguous`. PC-21 applied as written would have given `decrease` here; the sign is overridden (Gaps (c), (e)).
- *Turnover* (C-52). PC-7's If (the benefit "tied to keeping the good") is met only for moves beyond the county. The same two chains give `ambiguous`.

**The announcement window (C-12 to C-15, C-25).** Two timing chains run in opposite directions for seniors. Landlords end month-to-month tenancies before 1 October 2027 to re-let at the market or to hold units empty for withdrawal (C-13): moves rise. Qualifying seniors who planned a move within the county in those months gain the credit by waiting until 1 October (§7(a), "on or after the effective date"): moves fall. Signed together under RS-8 (0.9), the window is `ambiguous` for seniors (C-14). Tenants aged 62 to 64, or with under three years' tenure, gain by waiting until they qualify (C-15); after they qualify, their moves are reached by the chains of C-52 and are not signed separately. A no-cause termination in the window is not a timing shift of an action that also happens without the policy at another time: some would have happened later, and some happen only because the cap is coming, so the window is signed against the counterfactual as an increase (CR-12, 0.9).

**Withdrawals (C-19 to C-24).** §9 is a legal exit the policy creates (CR-6, 1.1), so pressure from the cap goes to it (CR-4, x: keep letting under the cap, y: withdraw and sell). Displaced households lose the option of staying (CR-1, operation 1) and must choose a home afresh. Some whose next-best home is outside Tessford, or outside the county, leave (A-14; 42% and 17% of displaced households did in 2019 to 2024). This is PC-21's subclass "whose sign runs the other way". Under U1-b displaced seniors are in the same position (C-23, C-24: increase). Under U1-a they keep the credit within the county: out of the county, the displacement chain (+) meets the credit's deterrence (−, as in C-10), with no dominance premise (C-24: ambiguous); out of Tessford, the displacement chain (+) meets the credit's means effect (kind 3) (C-23: ambiguous).

**The population caveat (PC-21).** PC-21 says it does not sign the area's population, since outsiders shut out of covered units settle elsewhere (PC-3, RS-4). That holds here, and the case adds two things. First, the shut-out side is not only newcomers: Tessford residents who form households (young adults leaving home, separations) and tenants of exempt units face fewer covered vacancies and higher exempt rents (C-31), and some settle in Corvell or Durnside (C-32). Their moves out of Tessford increase. Second, Tessford's households are not signed: insiders who keep their units stay more (C-9), while displaced households (C-22 to C-25) and shut-out residents (C-32) leave more, and newcomers who would have moved in settle elsewhere. No variable in 2.4 records moves into an area (Gaps (d)), so the newcomers' side is recorded only through the rents where they settle (C-29, C-30, C-31).

**County and Durnside rents (C-29, C-30).** The shut-out seekers push both up (RS-4). The credit adds two chains. Seniors who would have left the county and now move within it add demand there (C-10, CR-4). The credit's means effect can move qualifying seniors between Tessford and the rest of the county in either direction (as in C-11): a kind 3 chain on county demand. Its (−) direction is bounded by the case's figures: it can at most keep in Tessford the seniors who would have moved elsewhere in the county, about 115 households a year. With the premise that more shut-out seekers than that settle elsewhere in the county each year, and that county supply cannot expand at an unchanged cost in the medium horizon (A-20), the sign is `increase` (CR-12e: arithmetic with an empirical premise). In Durnside the credit pulls the other way: seniors who would have moved there now stay in the county. At most about 35 senior households a year moved to Durnside, so with A-21 the sign is `increase`. The County's landlords receive the credit as part of the rent they set; the credit is fixed by the vacated Tessford unit, not by the new rent, so it does not draw the new rent toward a limit (CR-6, 1.1, third-party payer, does not apply).

**Exempt units (C-31).** PC-8's Unless clause does not apply: withdrawn units cannot be re-let for five years (§9(d)) and leave the rental market for owner-occupation (C-33), so no covered landlord can move a unit into the exempt segment, and RS-13 adds no supply there. The new building that higher exempt rents bring (C-36) is a response to C-31 and cannot reverse it (CR-12e, response argument).

**Stopping.** C-30 and C-37 are at depth 3 and reach diffuse groups (Durnside renters; county landlords); the chains stop there. Condominium prices in Tessford are not signed: withdrawals add to their supply (C-33), while some seekers shut out of renting buy instead (CR-12b).

## 11. Report

**Stated aim.** The ordinance aims "to protect Tessford's tenants from displacement and to allow long-standing residents, especially older residents, to remain in their homes and in their community". Moves out of homes and out of the city are in scope and are recorded below. The value of staying in a "community" is not signed. This analysis says which way the moves go for each group, not whether the aim is met.

**The policy.** From 1 October 2027, the rent of a sitting tenant in a pre-1990 Tessford building may rise by at most about 1.8% a year; a new tenancy starts at whatever rent the landlord sets. Tenancies end only for cause and cannot be passed on. Owners may withdraw whole buildings on notice, paying each household $8,000 ($12,000 for seniors). A tenant aged 65 or over who leaves after three years keeps, as a payment to any landlord in Ambry County, the amount the cap would have saved them on the old home, recomputed each year; the payment ends if they leave the county. The City pays it from its general fund. A rent board funded by landlords' fees enforces the cap.

**Visible consequences.** Tenants who stay pay less than they would have from the medium horizon on, and the gap widens each year (C-1); landlords receive less by the same amount (C-3). In the first months the sign is not settled, because landlords raised rents at renewals before the base date (C-12, C-2). Seniors who leave for another home in the county pay its rent less the credit (C-4); the City's outlay rises each year (C-5).

**Invisible consequences.**
- *Sitting tenants who will not qualify for the credit* move less (C-8), leave Tessford less (C-9) and change jobs less (C-38). They get less maintenance (C-16, C-17); some sublet above the rent (C-18).
- *Qualifying seniors* leave Ambry County less (C-10). Whether they leave Tessford more or less, and whether they move out of their homes more or less, is not determined (C-11, C-52): see ambiguous effects.
- *Displaced tenants.* Some buildings are withdrawn and sold as condominiums (C-19, C-33); the covered stock shrinks (C-20). Their households must move (C-21) and some leave Tessford (C-22), with a relocation payment (C-46). In the months before the cap starts, some month-to-month tenants are given notice (C-13), and some of them leave Tessford (C-25). Taking all non-qualifying insiders together, those who keep their homes and those displaced, fewer leave Tessford in the medium term (C-49); in the long term the total is not determined (C-50).
- *People looking for a home in Tessford* find fewer covered vacancies and face longer searches, side payments or selection by landlords (C-6, C-7); fewer lettings happen (C-39). Exempt rents rise (C-31), and Tessford residents forming new households, and exempt tenants, leave the city more (C-32). Rents rise elsewhere in the county (C-29) and in Durnside (C-30).
- *Owners.* Covered buildings lose value from the moment the cap was expected (C-34); exempt and county rental buildings gain (C-35, C-37); new rental building rises if the exemption is trusted (C-36). Covered landlords pay registration and board costs (C-28) and have less to spend elsewhere (C-44).
- *The public side.* The general fund's payers fund the credit (C-40); the rent board and the County office use up staff time (C-42, C-43); the board's fees come from landlords (C-47). Lobbying over the new provisions rises (C-26).

**Ambiguous effects.**
- *Seniors and Tessford* (C-11, C-52, C-51). Within the county, the credit pays a qualifying senior the same amount whether they stay or move, so it works as added means rather than as a reason to stay. It may let some stay in Tessford who would have been priced out, or let others move to Corvell who would have stayed. What would decide it: how seniors with more means choose between Tessford and the rest of the county. For the same reason, the total of all insiders leaving Tessford is not determined (C-51).
- *Displaced seniors* (C-23, C-24) depend on U1. Under U1-b (no credit after a withdrawal), they leave Tessford and the county more. Under U1-a, the credit pulls the other way and the sign is not determined.
- *Seniors' moves in the months before the cap* (C-14): terminations push them up, waiting for the credit pushes them down.
- Sitting tenants' rent in the first months (C-2); their rent per unit of quality (C-48); lobbying over rent regulation as such (C-27).

**Key conditions.** That market rents keep rising faster than the cap (A-1, p = 0.9). That the forfeit of the gap outweighs what savings from it make affordable (A-27, p = 0.8): without it, the PC-21 and PC-7 signs are ambiguous. That the cut in moves out of the city by tenants who stay exceeds the moves of displaced tenants (A-26, p = 0.7): that is what makes the non-qualifying class total a decrease. That developers trust the exemption (A-24, p = 0.6).

**What this analysis cannot say.** How large any of these effects is; whether the gains to sitting tenants and credit holders outweigh the losses to seekers, owners and the general fund's payers; whether "community" is better kept; whether Tessford's population rises or falls (no variable records moves into an area); whether the policy is desirable. Health effects of moving in old age are outside version 1.

## Gaps found

**(a) Calculus gaps (a step no core rule covers).** None. Every step was derived from CR-1 to CR-12. The step that decided the seniors' signs, a payment attached equally to every option in a set, is CR-4's "a change in means alone" within the set and CR-4 proper across its boundary.

**(b) Unclear core rules.**
1. *CR-1 and CR-4, one payment attached to a set of options.* CR-1 (0.7) records a payment attached to an option as operation 3, and CR-4 treats "a change in means alone" as one that changes "the relative cost of no option". The credit does both: it changes no relative cost among the county options and changes every one of them against the options beyond the county. The analysis read CR-4 as applying per pair of options: CR-4 proper across the boundary of the set, "a change in means alone" within it. CR-4 could say so; it would also cover a grant paid only to residents, or a benefit kept only within an occupation.
2. *CR-4's exception for a benefit tied to staying.* The exception is written for a price rise or a lower return. A capped rent is the mirror case: a large gain in means for those who stay, part of which is saved and can later fund a move (C-9, C-10). It was applied with a dominance premise (A-27). The exception could name the mirror case of a benefit attached to one option.
3. *CR-12 0.8, kind 3 inside a class total.* Where one subclass's chain is kind 3 and the total is over the whole class (C-51), the clause gives `ambiguous` unless a premise covers both directions. It does not say whether arithmetic on the subclass's size can bound the kind 3 chain. Here it could for county rents (the most the means effect can withdraw is the counterfactual flow, about 115 a year; C-29) but not for the class total (the means effect can also add movers, with no bound in the figures).

**(c) Missing or wrong patterns.**
1. *PC-21 applied as written gave a sign that had to be overridden.* For qualifying seniors, PC-21 either applies (if "would not get an equal benefit at a destination outside the area" is read as "at some destination") and gives `relocation_out_of_area` decrease out of Tessford, where the derivation gives `ambiguous` (C-11); or its Unless clause blocks it ("the benefit is portable"), and the decrease out of the county (C-10) is not derived. Neither reading gives both results. See (e).
2. *PC-7 has the same binary If.* "The right ... is tied to keeping the good" is true for moves beyond the county and false within it; for qualifying seniors `turnover` is `ambiguous` (C-52), not `decrease`. PC-7 and RS-11 should carry the same per-destination clause as the proposed PC-21.
3. *PC-21 and PC-7 omit the CR-4 exception.* Both sign a decrease from the forfeit alone. The gap is a large share of the insider's means, and the savings it builds can fund a later move (CR-4 exception), so the decrease needs a dominance premise (A-27). No sign was overridden because the premise was stated, but neither pattern's Assumes line names it.
4. *PC-21's subclass split names only conversion and withdrawal (PC-5, RS-5).* Displacement also comes from the announcement window, where tenancies can still be ended without cause before eviction-for-cause starts (RS-8; C-13, C-25). Seen once; it fits in the proposed rewording.
5. *No pattern for a benefit portable within a range.* The credit was derived from CR-1, CR-4 and CR-8b. Candidate: **benefit portable over part of a region**. If: a benefit tied to a place or status follows its holder to some destinations and not others. Then: moves to destinations outside the range decrease (CR-4); moves between the original place and destinations within the range are a change in means alone (CR-4) and are not signed without a premise; moves within the range from beyond rise by diversion; demand in the range rises from diverted movers, and just beyond it falls (an opposing chain on prices there, C-30). Derivation: CR-1 (operation 3 on each option in the range), CR-4, CR-8b, CR-12. Seen once (WE-067).
6. No other pattern needed an override. PC-8's Unless clause did not apply (no supply added to the exempt segment); RS-7's Unless clause re-signed lobbying on the ordinance itself (C-27), which is the clause working.

**(d) Vocabulary.**
1. *`relocation_out_of_area` and two areas.* The variable is defined as moves out of "the area (city, region) the policy covers". Here the cap covers Tessford and the credit covers Ambry County, and both matter (C-10, C-11). It was used with a qualifier naming the area. Proposed wording: "out of a named area (the area a component covers, given as a qualifier)".
2. *No variable for moves into an area.* PC-21's population caveat (newcomers settle elsewhere) cannot be recorded even as a signed flow for newcomers; it was recorded only through rents where they settle (C-29 to C-31). A variable `relocation_into_area`, or households resident in an area, would let the caveat be stated as a consequence (for population, `ambiguous`).
3. *Instrument type for a payment conditional on residence in a range.* None of the subsidy types in 2.3 fits a payment attached to renting anywhere in a region after leaving a controlled unit; `lump_sum_subsidy` is closest, but the payment can be avoided by leaving the county, so it is operation 3, not operation 4 (CR-1, 0.7 and 1.1).
4. *A class defined by a policy-induced choice.* `seniors.credit` (seniors who have moved with the credit) is defined by a response to the policy; C-4 was signed per home rented against the same home without the policy. The ontology does not say how to sign the price paid by such a class.

**(e) Test of PC-21.**
- *If:* needed interpretation. "Would not get an equal benefit at a destination outside the area" is true of some destinations and false of others for qualifying seniors; it had to be read per destination set (section 5). It worked as written for non-qualifying insiders (C-9).
- *Then:* worked as written for insiders who keep the unit and cannot qualify (C-9), given the added premise A-27. Needed interpretation of the "two routes": "fewer of them are priced out" is not a second route but the same CR-4 move, since the gap is paid only to those who stay. That matters because where the benefit follows the holder, the "priced out" route turns into a means effect. Failed for qualifying seniors: applied as written it gives `decrease` out of Tessford, overridden to `ambiguous` (C-11).
- *Subclass split:* worked as written: displaced insiders' sign runs the other way (C-22), and the class total needed a CR-12 comparison and a premise on withdrawals (C-49, with A-26; long horizon `ambiguous`, C-50). Needed interpretation twice: displacement also comes from no-cause terminations in the announcement window (C-13, C-25), and the total over all insiders cannot be signed when one subclass's chain is kind 3 (C-51).
- *Population caveat:* worked as written: no population sign follows, and the shut-out settle elsewhere (C-29 to C-31). Needed interpretation: the shut-out side includes residents of the area (households forming in Tessford, exempt tenants), whose moves out are signed `increase` (C-32), and there is no variable for the newcomers' side ((d) 2).
- *Assumes:* needed interpretation. `mobility` worked (A-6). "The benefit is not portable to the destination" had to be stated per destination (A-7). A premise is missing: the CR-4 exception, settled here by A-27.
- *Unless:* failed. It treats portability as all or nothing. A benefit portable over part of the region does not block the pattern (moves beyond the range still decrease, C-10), and it does not let the pattern apply (moves into the range become a change in means and are `ambiguous`, C-11).

**Proposed rewording of PC-21.**
- **If:** PC-7 holds for a good tied to a place (a controlled tenancy in a city). Sort the insiders' destinations outside the area into those where the benefit, or an equal one, follows them (the portable range) and those where it is lost.
- **Then:** for `buyers.current` who keep the covered unit, `relocation_out_of_area` decrease toward every destination where the benefit is lost: leaving now forfeits the gap between the controlled and the market price (CR-3, CR-4). Insiders who would have been priced out are the same move, not a second route, because the gap is paid only to those who stay. Where nothing is portable, this is the whole of the sign. Where the benefit follows insiders over part of the region, sign two variables: moves out of the portable range decrease; moves out of the area are `ambiguous` unless a premise signs the means effect, since between staying and moving within the range the benefit is a change in means alone (CR-4). Moves within the range by those who would have left it rise. Insiders displaced by conversion, withdrawal or no-cause termination before the cap takes effect (PC-5, RS-5, RS-8) are a separate subclass whose sign runs the other way; the total for the class is a CR-12 comparison and needs a premise on the share displaced; a subclass whose own sign is `ambiguous` makes the total `ambiguous`. The pattern does not sign the area's population: outsiders who cannot get a unit settle elsewhere (PC-3, RS-4), and residents of the area who are shut out (households forming, tenants of exempt units) leave it more (PC-8, RS-4).
- **Assumes:** without the policy, some insiders would have left the area, priced out or for opportunities elsewhere (`mobility`); for each destination, whether the benefit follows the insider there (`other`); the forfeit of the future gap outweighs any move that savings built from the gap make affordable (CR-4 exception; `other`).
- **Unless:** the benefit is portable to every destination the insiders would consider (then the pattern gives no sign: the benefit is a change in means alone); the ceiling does not bind (PC-1).
- **Derivation:** CR-3 and CR-4 per pair of options (x: leave for destination d, y: stay), with CR-4's "a change in means alone" within the portable range and its exception for the size of the gap; CR-12 for the class total; against the no-policy price path (CR-12, 1.0). PC-7 and RS-11 should take the same per-destination clause.
