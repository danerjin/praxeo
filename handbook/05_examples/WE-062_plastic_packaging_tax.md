# WE-062 Tax on plastic packaging, with a recycled-content exemption and a small-firm threshold

Analyst: draft (Handbook 1.0 held-out test, corrected under 1.1)  |  Rule catalog version: 1.1  |  Status: awaiting expert review

A stylized, fictional policy in a fictional country. Several real countries tax plastic packaging by weight and exempt packaging above a recycled-content line; this one is not modeled on any of them. It is a held-out test of catalog 1.0 on a case none of the 1.0 changes were drawn from (they came from WE-046 to WE-055). It probes TX-2 and TX-7 where a tax is collected both from domestic manufacturers and at the border, on empty packaging and on the packaging of imported goods (a tax on the form of imports, with TX-8's 0.9 clause), RS-5 on redesign around a 30% recycled-content line, RS-21 where the tax counts tonnes but is not a cap, RS-17 and CR-1's 1.0 clauses on a 10-tonne notch measured over the previous year, CR-6's 1.0 clause that small effects in a wider market are signed (recycled and virgin resin, paperboard), RS-16's 1.0 clause on substitute prices per unit of a given quality, and CR-12's 1.0 clauses on the baseline of patterns and on what the response argument needs. The unclear provision is whether the 30% line is measured on each packaging component or averaged over a firm's output. The counterfactual throughout is **Saldria without the tax**: the same firms, rules and markets, with packaging choices as they would otherwise have been. Effects on the environment, litter and health are outside the scope of version 1. The probabilities are illustrative; the evidence column says what a real analysis would cite. After its core-only re-run (`05_examples_core/WE-062_plastic_packaging_tax_core.md`) it was corrected under catalog 1.1; the changes are listed just before "Gaps found".

## 1. Policy record

**Saldria Plastic Packaging Tax Act.** Saldria is a country of about 9.4 million people with its own currency, the taler (T), and a floating exchange rate. It produces no virgin plastic resin; all of it is imported, at world prices (about 1,150 T a tonne for the common grades). Saldria buys about 0.4% of the world's packaging resins. The figures:

| Of which imported empty | about 120,000 t |
| Of which arriving around imported goods | about 160,000 t |

Existing rules, unchanged on both paths: the Food Safety Authority's rule on recycled plastic in food contact, customs procedures, the general sales tax, and municipal collection contracts.

A campaign by environmental groups for a ban on single-use plastic packaging has run since 2023; a ban bill failed in 2025; the converters' association opposed it.

Unclear provisions:
- **U1, how the 30% is measured.** Section 4(1) speaks of "a component containing 30 per cent or more"; section 4(2) says the proportion is "determined over the components a person manufactures or imports in the quarter". Reading U1-a (per component): 4(2) fixes only the period over which each kind of component's content is measured by mass balance; each kind of component must itself reach 30% to be exempt. Reading U1-b (averaged): the proportion is the average over all the person's components in the quarter; if it is 30% or more, every component the person made or imported that quarter is exempt, and if not, none is. The Revenue Service has consulted and has not decided; no court has ruled. Readings get no probability; where a sign depends on one, each reading's sign is reported.

**Why TX-7 is not applied to the import leg.** TX-7's 1.1 Unless clause now settles this: where domestic output pays the same tax as imports, there are no protected sellers and the pattern does not apply. The reasoning under 1.0 is kept below. TX-7's If clause is "a tariff on an imported good". Taken alone, the tax collected at the border on imported packaging, and on the packaging of imported goods, meets it, and TX-7 would then conclude that domestic converters and fillers, as sellers of substitutes, gain. That conclusion compares the policy with another policy (imports taxed, domestic output not), not with Saldria without the tax (CR-12, 1.0, the baseline of patterns). Against no tax, domestic output pays the same 200 T a tonne. Collected at home and at the border, K1 is one tax on packaging placed on the Saldrian market and on packaging manufactured there for export. TX-2 is applied to it as a whole. The places where imports and domestic output are treated differently are derived separately: the small-firm threshold, which lets small users import packaging free of tax that they would pay on domestic packaging (C-37, C-55); the evidence rule in K2, which taxes imported packaging whose recycled content its foreign supplier cannot document (C-53, and on the domestic side C-65); and the absence of export relief (C-38, C-60, C-61).

**C-2, C-52, C-56: incidence.** TX-2 gives that buyers' price rises or sellers' net price falls, or both. DM-1 decides the split here (A-2): about a fifth of the packaging Saldria uses is imported empty, foreign converters have many other markets, and Saldria is a small buyer, so the price of each kind of packaging is set near the import price plus the tax, and packaging users bear more of the tax than sellers do. Because buyers bear more, their price rises (C-2). Buyers bearing more does not mean sellers bear nothing. CR-7b says that where both sides have some alternatives each bears part, and only the direction of each side's price is signed. A-2 says foreign converters absorb "little", not none, and domestic converters' plant is specific (A-20). So domestic converters' net price after tax on home sales falls (C-56). The fall is small, but CR-6's 1.0 clause says small effects are signed; the foreign converters' side is C-64. The price is per tonne of a given specification (RS-16, 1.0): a lighter or recycled design is a different specification, and a shift in the mix is not a price change. DM-1's horizon note points the same way over time: converters' capital is the redeployable side only slowly, so the burden shifts toward buyers as it does.

**C-3.** At the higher price, users buy less taxed packaging: they lighten designs (C-8), raise recycled content to reach the exemption (C-5), or switch to other materials (C-9) (CR-4c, A-3). The exchanges no longer made are a loss to both sides (OC-4, CR-5a), converters and foreign sellers on one side, users on the other.

**C-4.** The visible aim: components whose content can be raised to 30% at a cost below the tax are raised (CR-4, x: below 30% and taxed, y: at 30% and exempt; A-4). Recycled PET for food contact costs about 250 T a tonne more than virgin; at 30% that adds about 75 T to a tonne of bottles, against 200 T of tax. Some components already above 30% may cut their content toward the line when recycled resin becomes dearer (C-6, under U1-a), but that is a response to the price rise which the added demand itself causes, and it cannot reverse the rise in total recycled resin used (CR-12e, response argument; section 7). With total recycled tonnes up (C-26) and total taxed plastic down (C-3), the average rises; arithmetic under CR-12e covers ratios as well as levels (CR-12, 1.1).

**Redesign around the 30% line (C-5 to C-7).** RS-5 applies as written: the line divides covered from uncovered components, and components are reshaped to fall on the exempt side where that costs less than the tax (A-4). The material definition is a second line: a component is plastic packaging only if plastic is its heaviest material, so laminates and coated board can be redesigned to keep plastic just under half their weight (C-5). That is a different redesign, and it needs its own premise: that for some components it costs less than the tax saved (A-32). A-4 is about recycled content and does not cover it. C-5 holds if either premise holds (CR-12d: independent chains, same sign). Both are notches (CR-1, 1.0): a component at 29% pays tax on its whole weight, at 30% none, so components bunch just above the line.

The two readings differ on what happens beyond the line and across a firm's products.
- *Above the line (C-6).* On the counterfactual path some components carry more than 30% recycled content, some of them 50% or more for marketing. Under U1-a, content above 30% earns nothing from the tax, while recycled resin becomes dearer (C-27): where that content was chosen at the margin on cost (A-8), it is cut toward 30% (CR-4c), a decrease. Under U1-b, content above 30% on easy components (bottles, non-food tubs) lets a firm's average reach 30% while its films stay virgin, and so exempts its whole output (A-9). That chain raises content on easy components (+) against the dearer resin (−); no premise ranks them: `ambiguous`.
- *Across products (C-7).* Under U1-b the line is drawn over a person's whole quarterly output, so firm boundaries matter: a film converter that merges with, or registers its imports through, a bottle maker can exempt both (CR-4, x: separate, y: combined; RS-5 applied to organizations). Common control is counted together for K3, which helps here. Under U1-a, the exemption is decided component by component, and combining output changes nothing: `no_change` on this chain.
- *Components that cannot reach 30%.* Food-contact films cannot use recycled plastic (Food Safety Authority, on both paths). Under U1-a they pay tax on every tonne, so their users lighten them (C-8) or move to other materials (C-9). Under U1-b they escape the tax inside firms whose average reaches 30%, and pay it elsewhere. So under U1-b single-product film converters bear more of the tax than their multi-product rivals; under U1-a films pay whoever makes them, and the share is unchanged (C-57; CR-1 per reading, CR-7b). Under either reading, single-product film converters lose value (C-41).

**Penalties (C-58).** Firms caught underpaying or misdeclaring (C-19) pay penalties of 30% or more of the tax underpaid; the state receives them (CR-8b; A-17, A-1). They go into the general budget that also receives the tax and pays the plastics unit (CR-8, 1.0), and add to the net in C-49.

**RS-21 and the per-tonne count (C-8).** Under 1.0, RS-21's If clause was a cap, quota or right counted in physical units, and K1, a tax, did not meet it; C-8 was derived from CR-4c with the unit of the charge. RS-21's 1.1 clause now covers a tax counted in physical units and says that under a count by weight items become lighter. So RS-21 (1.1) is cited for C-8. The derivation is the same: the tax per component rises with its weight, so where a design can be lightened at a cost below the tax saved (A-5), it is (CR-4c). It is against the no-tax path, where the tonne carries no charge. For exempt components the weight is untaxed and this chain does not run.

**Substitutes (C-9).** Paper and board, glass and metal serve some of the same ends (A-6); users move to them where the tax exceeds the cost of switching (RS-1, CR-4). Their prices are section 6's question (RS-16).

**The 10-tonne notch (C-12, C-13, C-18).** A firm with 9.9 t in 2027 pays nothing in 2028; one with 10.1 t pays 200 T on every chargeable tonne, at least 2,000 T, plus registration and records (2,500 to 4,000 T a year). Crossing costs about 5,000 T a year or more (CR-1, 1.0: the jump is a cost of crossing). RS-17 says that firms near the line stay below it. Its main clause is phrased "just below the threshold increases relative to just above", a comparison between two bands; its 1.1 baseline clause says to re-sign that against no policy (CR-12, 1.0), as was done here. Each band is signed separately: firms that would have handled 10 to 13 t keep their tonnage under 10 t (A-14), so the count just below rises (C-12) and the count just above falls (C-13). Because the line is measured over the previous year, the response begins in 2027, from the announcement in March (RS-17; CR-10a). RS-17's other move, splitting activity across separately counted units, is limited by the common-control rule: only moving the importer of record to unrelated parties (customers, foreign sellers) remains, and A-15 (p = 0.4) is below the cut-off, so it is not reported. RS-17's 1.0 clause on eligibility does not apply: no program is lost at the line.

For firms near the line, two timing chains meet in 2027 (C-18). Bringing purchases forward before the tax starts (RS-8, A-16) raises 2027 tonnage (+), but that would push some of them over the line for 2028; those staying under cut 2027 tonnage instead (−). The class contains both, so the sign is `ambiguous` (RS-8, 0.9: more timing chains; CR-12b).

**Small users importing directly (C-55).** K3 exempts a small firm on what it imports, but a small firm that buys from a domestic converter pays the tax in the price (C-2), since the converter is liable. Small users who can import their own packaging at a cost below the tax (A-30: 5 t a year saves 1,000 T, against minimum order sizes and freight) do so (RS-5: the actor reshapes who imports; CR-4). This is a burden on domestic converters' sales to small users that their foreign rivals selling directly to those users do not bear: the reverse of a tariff, for one class of buyers.

**Timing for liable firms (C-15 to C-17).** Firms well above the line, which will pay in 2028 whatever they do, bring purchases and production forward into late 2027 and carry larger stocks into January (RS-8, CR-7a across time; A-16). Each window is signed against the counterfactual (02_ontology, 2.5).

**Evasion (C-19).** Recycled content is hard to verify chemically and rests on declarations and mass-balance records. Where misdeclaring costs less than the tax, given the audit rate and penalties, some firms misdeclare (RS-3, A-17). This is breach within the legal market, `legal_breach`, not `informal_activity`; it rises from none, since there is no tax to evade on the counterfactual path.

**Compliance (C-20).** TX-5. Registration and records are a cost fixed per firm (CR-4, 0.8): they bear on whether, and at what size, a firm handles packaging itself, which is why they add to the notch, not on the price per tonne.

**Lobbying (C-21).** CR-4's 1.0 clause and RS-7's 1.0 clause apply: lobbying is signed against the counterfactual contest. U1 and the implementing rules (evidence of recycled content, definitions of "substantial modification") are questions that exist only under the Act, so lobbying on them rises from none (CR-12, 0.9; A-18). CR-4's 1.1 clause on "the same question" confirms this: lobbying over provisions that exist only under the policy is an increase, while the contested question on the counterfactual path is the policy field (plastic packaging rules, the ban). Multi-product firms press for U1-b, recyclers for the reading they expect to raise demand for their resin. Lobbying over a ban on single-use plastic, which was contested on the counterfactual path, is not signed: whether the tax makes the ban campaign and its opponents spend more or less needs a premise the case lacks (A-28, p = 0.45, below the cut-off).

**Investment (C-22 to C-25).** Converters invest in drying, decontamination and blending lines to use recycled resin (C-22; A-27). Converting capacity (C-23) meets two chains. TX-3 and RS-6 give a decrease for the taxed activity, but the taxed activity is packaging placed on the market from all sources, not domestic converting. The evidence rule taxes imported components without documented recycled content as 0% (A-29), so users move from undocumented imports to domestic components with documented content, which are exempt (C-65; CR-4, CR-9). That is a (+) chain on domestic converting. It needs A-29 (p = 0.65), so it is a kind 2 chain with p of at least 0.5 (CR-12, 0.8), and C-23 is `ambiguous` without a dominance premise; none is available. The total (C-24) combines an increase with an `ambiguous` item, so it is `ambiguous` (CR-12, 0.8: kind 3). Recyclers expand (C-25), if they expect the tax and its exemption to last (A-19).

**Recycled resin (C-26 to C-30).** More components need recycled resin (C-5), so at the old price users wish to buy more than recyclers and importers wish to sell (CR-9 with LW-10; CR-6). Domestic supply is limited by collection, which is near the capacity of current routes and sorting lines; more resin comes from added collection and from the region only at a higher cost (A-10). So in the short run the quantity delivered rises and the price rises (C-26, C-27; CR-6, 0.9: the split between price and quantity rests on A-10). In the medium run recyclers expand (C-25) and imports grow. CR-12e's response argument says these responses cannot push the price back below its old level, since expansion stops once yields are equal. Under CR-12's 1.0 clause it gives `increase` only with three things stated. *The total it bounds:* recycled resin delivered in Saldria from all sources, domestic and imported. *A premise that cost per unit does not fall as the activity expands:* collecting more means reaching more dispersed sources and accepting dirtier bales, and the regional price itself rises (C-30); A-10 states this rising-cost premise. *A partial response:* supply cannot fully expand at the old cost within the medium run, also A-10. Imports, and recyclers' entry, are responses to the same price rise; once the total is named over both sources they are inside the argument, not a separate chain. CR-12's 1.1 clause now says so: responses within the named total (imports answering the same price) are part of the response, and only responses through other markets are second chains (Gaps found, (b) 1, settled). No stock built in advance holds the price down: recycled resin is not stockpiled for the tax. So C-28 is an increase. The long run is not signed: plants have fixed costs, and with enough expansion the cost per tonne could fall.

Users who used recycled resin anyway (C-29) pay more for it and gain nothing from the exemption, which they would have met without the tax. They are worse off by their own ranking (CR-3a), and, since they keep buying, have less to spend on other things (CR-3, note). Municipalities sell their bales for more (C-33) and in time collect more (C-34; A-26).

**Small effects in wider markets (C-30, C-31, C-10).** CR-6's 1.0 clause: a chain that moves a price in a wider market is signed, with its size in the evidence column. Saldria buys about 3% of the regional recycled-resin market: the added demand raises the regional price (C-30), small. It buys about 0.4% of the world's packaging resins: the fall in its virgin resin purchases (C-32) lowers the world price (C-31), very small. Paperboard is traded regionally: the move to board raises its regional price (C-10), small, and more than that at the domestic mills while they run near capacity (A-7).

**Substitute prices per unit of quality (C-10, C-11).** RS-16 as written, with its 1.0 clause. The price is per tonne of a given grade of board and per container of a given size and type. Users moving from plastic tend to buy coated, barrier-grade board, which costs more per tonne than plain grades. The average price of board bought in Saldria would rise from that change in mix alone, and the mix change is not counted. The policy attaches no cost to the substitutes, so RS-16's clause on costs on the substitute does not apply; and it adds no supply to their markets, so the Unless clause (RS-13) does not either. Glass containers are costly to transport, so the domestic plant's price rises while it runs at 88% capacity and a new furnace is two years away (C-11, short run only). Existing users of board and glass (`buyers.substitutes`) pay the higher prices too, and at the higher price they buy less while capacity is fixed (C-63; CR-4c; the capacity drawn to packaging is withdrawn from their uses, CR-8a). With a higher yield on board and glass capacity, and the tax expected to last (A-19), the mills and the glass plant add capacity in the medium run (C-62; CR-7a, CR-10a, CR-10b: a furnace takes two years, A-7).

**Packaged goods (C-35, C-36).** For goods in which packaging is 6% to 10% of the price, RS-14 applies: the input's cost rises, and the sellers' competitors face the same increase, because imported goods pay the tax on their packaging too, apart from the small importers' 4% share. So the price rise dominates (C-35; A-11), and at the higher price buyers buy less (C-36; CR-4c). Some of them move to loose produce and to tap or refill water, which serve the same ends for some buyers (C-66; RS-1, CR-4; A-33). For goods in which packaging is 1% to 3% of the price, the sign is the same but its premise (that packaging is a "material part" of cost, A-11) is weaker. Those goods are not reported separately.

**Imports by form (C-37, C-53, C-54) and the currency (C-40).** The tax changes the form of Saldria's imports as well as their quantity:
- virgin resin imports fall (C-32);
- recycled resin, board and glass imports rise (C-26, C-9);
- empty packaging imports fall. They are taxed like domestic output, and imports whose recycled content the foreign supplier cannot document are taxed as 0% (A-29). Small users who import directly push the other way (C-55); they handle at most 25,000 t in all, and A-31 states that their shift is smaller than the fall in liable firms' imports (CR-12e, an empirical premise). So C-54 is a decrease. Foreign converters, with many other markets, absorb only a little of the tax on what they still sell to Saldria, but some (A-2), so their net price before tax falls, slightly (C-64; CR-7b, CR-6 1.0: small effects are signed). The imports taxed as 0% for want of evidence lose sales to domestic components whose 30% content is documented and so exempt (C-65; CR-1, CR-4 for users, CR-9; A-29, A-4): the domestic side of the evidence rule;
- imported packaged goods: three chains with opposite signs. The tax on all packaged goods lowers purchases (C-36, −). The evidence rule falls on foreign suppliers who cannot document their content (A-29, −). Goods brought in by small importers are untaxed and gain against taxed rivals (C-37, +; A-22). No premise ranks them: `ambiguous` (C-53).

So imports of some goods fall and of others rise. TX-8's 0.9 clause applies: where a policy reduces imports of one good and raises imports of others made from it or used in its place, the currency chain is itself `ambiguous` (kind 3) unless a premise says which dominates. Exports of packaging and packaged goods also fall (C-38). By the mirror case, that gives residents fewer foreign means for imports, and, by CR-9's 0.7 clause, leaves foreign buyers' means to other exporters, a (+) chain for them. The effect on other export industries is `ambiguous` (C-40; A-21 is the premise that trade is not offset by capital flows within the medium run). TX-8 has no 1.0 clause; the 0.9 one worked as written.

**Exporters (C-38, C-39).** The Act taxes packaging that is exported, empty or filled. Exporters sell at prices set abroad (A-12), against foreign rivals who pay no Saldrian tax, so they cannot pass it on. RS-14's "competitors exempt" case applies: the quantity effect dominates (C-38; RS-6, CR-7b). With the foreign price fixed (A-12), the tax comes out of what exporters keep: converters' net price on exports of empty packaging after tax falls (C-60), and so does fillers' net price on exported packaged goods, after the tax in their packaging (C-61; CR-9, CR-7b). Over the long run, some export filling lines are replaced abroad rather than at home (C-39; RS-4, TS-1; A-13). That is a move across the boundary which the tax favors. TS-6 says it is hard to reverse.

**Asset values (C-41 to C-43, C-68).** Expected from the March 2027 announcement (CR-7c, CR-10c). Converting plants are specific (A-20). For plants serving exports (the tax on exports comes out of what they keep, A-12) and for single-product food-film plants, no exempt sale offsets the fall in taxed output, and their value falls (C-41). Single-product film converters lose most, under either reading: under U1-a they pay on every tonne, and under U1-b they compete with multi-product rivals whose films are exempt. For the other converting plants, the fall in taxed output (A-3) meets the evidence-rule chain, which moves users from undocumented imports to their documented exempt components (C-65; A-29, p = 0.65, a kind 2 chain): `ambiguous` without a dominance premise (C-68). Recycling plants (C-42) and board, glass and can makers (C-43) gain. Each assumes investors expect the tax to last (A-19). For firms near the 10-tonne line, see section 7 (C-14).

**Labor (C-44, C-45, C-59).** Labor follows output as a group of staff and hours (CR-6, 1.1). For converters, lower taxed tonnage, lower exports and small users importing their own packaging push it down (TX-3, RS-6, OC-5; A-3, A-12, A-25); the move of users from undocumented imports to documented domestic components (C-65, A-29), and the work of lightening and of handling recycled resin, push it up. The (+) chain is kind 2 with p of at least 0.5, so C-44 is `ambiguous` (CR-12b). In late 2027, production brought forward (C-15) raises converters' labor in that window, in staff or hours (C-59; RS-8, CR-9 with LW-10; A-16, A-25); the tax and the evidence rule are not yet in force then. The early-2028 window is not signed separately: there the stock drawdown (C-16) meets the evidence-rule chain, as in C-44. Recyclers and substitute makers expand existing activities, so the group form applies to them too, as at_least_one_increase (C-45; OC-5).

**Certification (C-46).** K2's evidence rule creates demand for certificates and mass-balance audits (CR-9). Some certification exists on the counterfactual path for marketing claims, so this is an increase on an existing activity, not one from none.

**Stopping points.** Suppliers of converters' equipment, the workers of export industries (depth 4 through the currency), and municipal taxpayers who gain from bale receipts are not followed further.

**Checks.** RS-9: no shopping trips are moved. RS-10: no trade goes underground; misdeclaration (C-19) stays in the legal market. RS-11: no benefit is tied to a place. RS-12: no complement in use is identified. RS-13: the policy adds no supply to a linked market. RS-24 (1.1) was considered and not applied: the evidence and testing costs fixed per component (C-46, C-51) attach to claiming the exemption or to a redesign, not to offering the item, which can still be sold taxed; the cost of offering it is per tonne. PC-20 does not apply: nothing caps what buyers pay.

## 8. Time structure

- **Announcement to enactment (March to June 2027).** Values move: converters down, recyclers and substitute makers up (C-41 to C-43). Firms near the 10-tonne line start managing their 2027 tonnage, since it decides 2028 liability (C-12, C-13, C-18). Lobbying on U1 begins (C-21).
- **Second half of 2027.** Converters order recycled-resin equipment (C-22) and redesign components (C-5); recycled resin contracts for 2028 are written at higher prices (C-27). In the last quarter, liable firms bring purchases forward and build stocks (C-15, C-17), and converters use more labor to make the extra output (C-59).
- **2028.** Liable firms draw down stocks in the first quarter (C-16). Recycled resin, board and glass prices are highest while capacity is fixed (C-10, C-11, C-27). Exporters lose sales (C-38).
- **Medium run.** Board and glass capacity is added (C-62). Recyclers expand and collection grows (C-25, C-34), and the recycled resin price stays above its counterfactual level by A-10 (C-28). Converters' capacity shrinks (C-23). Under U1-b, firms reorganize to average (C-7). The 30% line and the definitions settle into product design; habits of buying change gradually (TS-8).
- **Long run.** Export lines are replaced abroad (C-39), which is hard to reverse (TS-6). The rate is fixed in talers and not indexed (TS-3). TS-3's 1.1 clause asks for the expected path of prices as a premise: A-34 states that Saldrian prices are expected to rise over the long run. Whether the recycled resin price stays above its counterfactual level in the long run is not signed (section 6).

## 11. Report

**The policy.** From 1 January 2028, Saldria taxes plastic packaging at 200 talers a tonne, collected from domestic makers and from importers of empty packaging and of packaged goods, with no relief for exports. Packaging with at least 30% recycled plastic is exempt; whether that is judged component by component or averaged over a firm's quarterly output is unclear (U1). Firms that handled under 10 tonnes in the previous year pay nothing; above that, they pay on everything.

**Visible consequences.** The state collects the tax (C-1). Taxed packaging costs its users more (C-2), and users bear more of the tax than sellers (C-52), though domestic and foreign converters keep slightly less per tonne too (C-56, C-64). Less taxed plastic is used (C-3), and the average recycled content of packaging rises (C-4).

**Invisible consequences.**
- *Design.* Components are redesigned to reach 30% and composites to keep plastic under half their weight (C-5); taxed components get lighter (C-8); users move to board, glass and metal (C-9). Under U1-a, components that carried more than 30% recycled content cut it toward the line; under U1-b the sign is open (C-6). Under U1-b only, firms combine product lines to average (C-7), and single-product film converters bear more of the tax than multi-product rivals (C-57).
- *Recycled resin.* More is used, and it costs more in Saldria, in the short and medium run (C-26 to C-28). Users who already met 30% pay more for it and gain nothing (C-29). The regional price rises slightly (C-30); the world price of virgin resin falls very slightly (C-31), and Saldria imports less of it (C-32). Municipalities sell bales for more and collect more (C-33, C-34). Recyclers invest (C-25) and gain value (C-42).
- *Substitutes.* Board and glass cost more per unit of a given grade (C-10, C-11), also for those who used them anyway, who buy less of them while capacity is fixed (C-63). Their makers gain value, hire and add capacity (C-43, C-45, C-62).
- *Converters.* Plants serving exports and single-product film plants lose value (C-41); for the others the value is open (C-68). They invest in recycled-resin equipment (C-22). Domestic components with documented 30% content gain sales from imports taxed for want of evidence (C-65). Converters use more labor in late 2027 (C-59).
- *Small firms.* Firms near 10 t keep under the line, starting in 2027 (C-12, C-13). Small users import packaging directly rather than buy taxed domestic packaging (C-55), and small importers' goods gain sales (C-37).
- *Timing.* Liable firms stock up in late 2027 and buy less in early 2028 (C-15 to C-17).
- *Trade.* Exporters, taxed with no relief, keep less per unit exported (C-60, C-61), sell less abroad (C-38) and in time replace export lines abroad (C-39). Imports of empty packaging fall (C-54).
- *Buyers.* Goods with a high packaging share cost more and sell less (C-35, C-36); some buyers move to loose produce and tap or refill water (C-66).
- *Compliance and enforcement.* Registration and records (C-20), redesign and testing (C-51), certification (C-46), the Revenue Service's work (C-48), misdeclaration (C-19) and the penalties it brings (C-58), and lobbying on U1 (C-21). Revenue falls short of the static projection (C-50); net of the appropriation, the budget gains (C-49), and someone among other taxpayers, recipients of other spending or future taxpayers gains it (C-67). Those who keep using taxed plastic have less to spend elsewhere (C-47).

**Ambiguous effects.** Converters' investment in capacity and in total (C-23, C-24), their labor (C-44) and the value of converting plants other than export and single-product film plants (C-68): lower taxed tonnage pushes them down, and the move from undocumented imports to documented domestic components pushes them up; the value of firms near the 10-tonne line (C-14) and their 2027 purchases (C-18); imports of packaged goods (C-53); other export industries, through the currency (C-40); under U1-b, recycled content of components already above 30% (C-6).

**Key conditions.** That users can cut taxed plastic at a cost below the tax (A-3, p = 0.85) and that some components can reach 30% cheaply (A-4, p = 0.85) drive most results. The rise in the recycled resin price into the medium run rests on rising collection costs (A-10, p = 0.7). Incidence on users rests on imports at the margin (A-2, p = 0.75). The evidence rule's effect on domestic converters rests on undocumented imports (A-29, p = 0.65). Composite redesign rests on A-32 (p = 0.65).

**What this analysis cannot say.** How much plastic or revenue changes; whether the tax reduces litter or emissions, which is outside the scope of version 1; whether users or converters bear the larger loss in money; whether the policy is desirable.

## Gaps found

**(a) Calculus gaps (a step no core rule covers).** None. Every step was derived from CR-1 to CR-12, with patterns where their preconditions held.

**(b) Unclear core rules.**
1. *(Settled in 1.1: CR-12's 1.1 clause says responses within the named total are part of the response.)* *CR-12 1.0, what the response argument needs: "a second response to the same change".* The clause says a second response to the same change is a second chain to combine. For the recycled resin price (C-28), recyclers' expansion and imports from the region both respond to the same price rise. Read literally, imports would be a second chain with no dominance premise, and the medium-run price would be `ambiguous`. But neither response, nor both together, can push the price below its old level once the total is named over both sources and each has rising cost. The clause's examples (a new competitor attracted by the policy, a stock built in advance) are changes that are not responses to the price itself. I read "second response" as a response to a different change the policy causes, or one outside the named total. The clause should say that responses to the same price movement are covered together once the total includes them all.
2. *(Settled in 1.1: CR-1's 1.1 clause on a line on a ratio.)* *CR-1 1.0, notches on a ratio.* Under U1-b the notch is on a firm's average recycled content, not on a size. It can be crossed by raising the numerator (more recycled resin) or by changing the denominator (adding high-content output, dropping virgin lines). CR-1's notch note ("record the operation on each side, and the jump as a cost of crossing") covers it, but RS-17 and CR-1 are written for a line on a quantity. A sentence that a line on a ratio is crossed by moving either term would help.
3. *CR-6 1.0, small effects are signed.* Worked as written, but in a chain with several links the "small" effect can sit at different depths (Saldria's tonnage, then virgin resin demand, then the world price). The clause does not say whether the depth limit of workflow step 6 still applies to such chains. C-31 is at depth 3.

**(c) Missing or wrong patterns.**
1. *(Settled in 1.1: TX-7's Unless clause.)* *TX-7 on a border tax matched by an equal domestic tax.* TX-7's preconditions are met by the import leg taken alone (a tax collected on imported goods). Its conclusion, that domestic sellers of substitutes gain, is wrong against the no-tax counterfactual when domestic output pays the same rate. Applied as written it gives a sign I had to override (section 2; CR-12, 1.0, the baseline of patterns). TX-7 needs an Unless clause: where domestic output of the same good pays the same tax, apply TX-2 to the whole tax, and derive gains or losses to domestic sellers only from the points where imports and domestic output are treated differently. Here those points ran the other way: the small-firm threshold favors imports by small users (C-55, C-37), the evidence rule disfavors undocumented imports (C-53), and the lack of export relief burdens exporters (C-38).
2. *(Settled in 1.1: RS-21's clause on charges counted in units; now cited for C-8.)* *RS-21 and a tax counted in physical units.* RS-21 is written for a cap, quota or right. A per-tonne tax prices the counted unit as a cap's lease price does, and CR-4c gives the same move: the value carried per counted unit rises. But RS-21's examples sign `unit_size` as an increase (larger aircraft per slot). With a count by weight and a free choice of items per tonne, the item gets lighter (C-8). RS-21 could be widened to "a charge or cap counted in physical units", and say that the item moves so as to raise value per counted unit, which under a weight count means less weight per item.
3. *(Settled in 1.1: RS-17's baseline clause.)* *RS-17's main clause is phrased against the other band.* "Just below the threshold increases relative to just above" is a comparison between two bands, not with the counterfactual. Signed against no tax, the band below rises and the band above falls (C-12, C-13). The 1.0 preamble says to re-sign such phrasing; RS-17 could be reworded.
4. *A threshold that exempts small importers but not domestic sellers.* K3 exempts a small firm on what it imports, while its domestic supplier is liable. This works as a negative tariff on domestic sales to small buyers (C-55). No pattern prompts it; it was derived from CR-1 (the operation depends on who is the importer) and CR-4. Candidate pattern: "a size exemption for the person liable moves liability toward the exempt party's own imports".
5. No other pattern gave a sign I had to override. *(Corrected after the core-only comparison: TX-3 and RS-6 were misapplied to domestic converting as if it were the whole taxed activity; the patterns are right as written, the error was the analyst's.)*

**(d) Vocabulary.**
1. No variable for the distribution of a product attribute around a line (bunching of recycled content at 30%); recorded through `reclassification` (C-5) and `product_attribute` with a qualifier (C-6).
2. No actor class for a firm defined by its size against a threshold; `firms.near_threshold` was coined, as in WE-048.
3. `tax_share_borne` is defined between subgroups "in the same market"; here buyers are compared with domestic and foreign sellers together (C-52).
