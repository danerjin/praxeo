# 7. Sources

The texts the Handbook cites, the editions used, and how each citation was checked.

## How the citations were checked

Every citation in `01_foundations.md`, `04a_core_rules.md` and `04_rules.md` was checked against the texts in October 2026. Each was checked in one of three ways:

- **[d] direct:** the text itself was opened and read.
- **[s] search extract:** the chapter and section were confirmed from the publisher's online edition (chapter and section titles in the page addresses and extracts), but the full page was not opened. Quotations marked [s] are close, and should be checked against the page before they are printed.
- **[u] unverified:** the source could not be checked. The citation is the best available, and must be checked before it is relied on.

The online editions of Mises, Rothbard and Bastiat (mises.org, econlib.org) could not be opened directly from the working environment, so most citations are [s]. Opening them directly is the first task for whoever reviews this file.

The two Hazlitt editions number their chapters differently from ch. 18 onward:
- **Chapters 1 to 17** are the same in both: "The Lesson", "The Broken Window", ... "Government Price-Fixing".
- **1946:** ch. 18 is "Minimum Wage Laws" and the book ends at ch. 24, "The Lesson Restated".
- **1979:** ch. 18 is "What Rent Control Does", ch. 19 is "Minimum Wage Laws", and ch. 26 is "The Lesson After Thirty Years".

The 1946 edition has no chapter on rent control.

## Findings from the check

- **Cost is defined in HA ch. IV**, not ch. VII. Ch. VII is marginal utility and the law of returns.
- **P&M ch. 1 is "Defense Services on the Free Market"**, which is the wrong place for the mutual benefit of exchange. That is P&M ch. 2 §2.
- **The tailor is Hazlitt's** (EIOL 1946 ch. 2). Bastiat's example of the forgone purchase is shoes or a book. OC-5 is renamed accordingly.
- **The iron law of prohibition is Richard Cowan's** (1986), not "Cowen".
- **The rent control chapter exists only in the 1979 edition** of Hazlitt. Its arguments on maintenance are cited at second hand only.
- **"Incidence follows the alternatives" is not Rothbard's position.** DM-1, and the incidence clause of CR-7b, state the standard treatment. Rothbard's first law of incidence holds that no tax can be shifted forward: the burden falls on the incomes of original factors, and any later rise in price comes about through reduced supply, which he classes as an effect of the tax rather than shifting. The two agree on what happens (the burden first falls on those who cannot escape it, and prices rise only as supply contracts) but describe it differently. The disagreement is recorded under DM-1 and in Q3.
