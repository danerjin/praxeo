# WE-036 / Analysis: Employer health cover mandate ("play or pay")

Analyst: core-only re-run (Handbook 0.8)  |  Rules: core rules CR-1 to CR-12 only  |  Status: compared

## 1. Policy record

**Quenby Fair Share Health Cover Act.** The State of Quenby has about 5.1 million people and 2.2 million private-sector employees. Its largest city, Marisport, lies on the border with the State of Tarnley, which has no such law; about 35,000 people commute across the border each day. Quenby's minimum wage is $14.00 an hour, indexed to prices; about 11% of private employees earn at or near it, mostly in retail, food service, cleaning, security and care.

Firms with 50 or more FTE employ about 1.45 million people, of whom about 1.15 million average 30 or more hours a week. Of those, about 79% (910,000) are already offered a plan that meets the Act's standard with an employer share of at least 70%; about 9% (105,000) are offered a plan that falls short; about 12% (135,000) are offered nothing. The 135,000 work at about 2,100 firms: about 62,000 at 45 large firms each with more than 100 such employees, and about 73,000 at about 2,050 mid-size firms (about 36 per firm). About 1,300 firms have 40 to 49 FTE and about 1,100 have 50 to 60. About 380 firms (farms, food processing, resorts, landscaping) have 30 to 49 year-round FTE and would reach 50 if seasonal hours counted.

Arithmetic from the record: a 70% share of the $7,400 standard premium is $5,180 a year, 17.8% of full-time minimum-wage pay ($2.49 an hour at 40 hours, $3.32 at 30). The $3,000 charge is $1.44 an hour at 40 hours and $1.92 at 30. For a capped firm with 1,500 uncovered full-timers the $300,000 cap is $200 a head. The 45 capped firms together owe at least 45 x $300,000 = $13.5 million a year, against the $4.1 million appropriation.

Existing rules, in force in the counterfactual too: R1 minimum wage ($14.00, indexed); R2 exclusion of employer premium contributions from the 4.5% income tax (wages taxed); R3 Indigent Care Program (about $310 million a year from the general fund, paid to hospitals that claim for uninsured residents below 200% of poverty; about $9 million administration); R4 overtime at 1.5 times above 40 hours a week.

Unclear provisions:
- U1, seasonal workers in the K3 count. U1-a: they count by their hours. U1-b: they are left out of the count. Under both readings a seasonal worker averaging 30+ hours at a bound firm is owed an offer after 60 days.

Counterfactual: Quenby without the Act, with R1 to R4 in place. Counterfactual law for CR-1: an employer may employ a full-timer with or without any health plan, with no charge either way.

K1 gives each bound employer two ways to comply. For each uncovered full-timer the employer compares the cost of offering (the 70% share times the share of workers who take it up, since a declined offer counts) with $3,000 (CR-4, x: offer, y: pay). Neither is free, so both are worse than the counterfactual for the employer (CR-3a). The minimum wage (R1) is the existing rule that holds the money wage of low-paid workers when the new cost would otherwise be passed back in wages.

## 8. Time structure

- **Introduction to effective date (9 Nov 2026 to 1 Jan 2028).** Business values move when the Act becomes expected, from introduction (A-21): bound firms down (C-37), small unbound firms up (C-39), capped, near-threshold and compliant firms ambiguous (C-38, C-46, C-47), seasonal firms per reading (C-50). This is CR-7c with CR-10c.
- **Measurement year 2027.** Status for 2028 is set by average FTE over 2027, which owners know from signing (A-15). The effect on firms near 50 therefore starts in 2027, a year before any charge is paid (C-22, C-49; CR-10a). A-31: part of the growth held back is delayed rather than lost. Path note (CR-12h): relative to the counterfactual, the employment of these firms is lower in 2027 and later; delayed growth narrows the gap after 2028 but does not put employment above the counterfactual in any window, because without the Act the firms would already have grown. No window has a positive sign, so there is no timing-shift consequence with opposite signs.
- **Wages.** Short run (2028): money wages fixed by pay scales (A-3); employers bear the cost (C-6, C-18). Medium and long run: raises are smaller (A-6), so part of the cost moves to workers (C-17, C-19, C-20, C-34). The full-time employment effect grows as wages adjust and as staffing is redesigned (C-14).
- **Minimum-wage workers.** R1 is indexed to prices, so it stays binding where it binds (A-9); the CR-6 margins (C-10) persist.
- **Durability.** Rescheduled jobs, contracting arrangements and firm sizes are stocks that change through hiring and contract renewal (CR-10b), so C-11, C-23 and C-24 build up over the medium run. The Act has no expiry.
- **ICP and care.** Effects on billed care and the ICP start when cover starts, after 60 days of employment in 2028 (C-31, C-32).

## 11. Report

**The policy:** From 1 January 2028, every Quenby employer with an average of 50 or more full-time equivalents in the previous year must, for each employee averaging 30 or more hours a week, either offer a health plan meeting a state standard and pay at least 70% of the employee-only premium, or pay the state $3,000 a year for that employee, up to $300,000 a firm. An offer that is declined counts. Status for 2028 is set by the 2027 average.

**Visible consequences:** Some full-timers at large firms who had no cover or poor cover are now offered a qualifying plan (C-1, C-2), and some short plans are upgraded (C-3). The state collects charges from firms that pay instead (C-4). Insurers sell more group plans (C-29).

**Invisible consequences:**
- *Bound employers:* In the short run, while wages are fixed, they bear the cost (C-6, C-18); payers have less for other uses (C-5). Every bound firm, compliant or not, files quarterly offer reports (C-7). They reschedule some 30 to 35 hour jobs under 30 hours (C-11, C-15), get more hours from existing full-timers (C-13), and employ fewer full-time heads (C-14); total heads move either way (C-16). Some misreport offers (C-25). Values of non-compliant bound firms fall from the bill's introduction (C-37). Mid-size payers lose sales to rivals that do not bear the per-head cost (C-41), and some take workers through capped staffing agencies (C-24).
- *Workers:* Workers at or near the minimum wage cannot have the cost passed back in their wage, so they lose full-time jobs or other terms (C-10). Workers whose jobs are rescheduled lose hours (C-12). Over the medium run, wages above the minimum grow more slowly at bound firms (C-17), workers bear more of the burden than employers (C-19), workers at payers have less to spend (C-20) and some move to small firms, part-time work or Tarnley (C-21). Newly covered workers change jobs less while their wages are fixed (C-27).
- *Firms near the threshold:* Firms at 40 to 60 FTE hold employment below 50 from 2027 (C-22) and contract work out (C-23, C-43).
- *Capped chains:* They pay a fixed $300,000 that does not change their hiring at the margin, and they gain sales and agency business from mid-size rivals, so they employ more full-timers (C-42).
- *Other markets:* Small firms gain value (C-39) and sales (C-40); Tarnley producers gain sales from Quenby tradable producers (C-44, C-45); individual-plan insurers lose customers (C-30); more care is billed to insurers (C-31) and the ICP pays out less (C-32).
- *The state:* Departments spend $4.1 million a year (C-8); in the short run charges exceed it (C-33); over time the income tax base shrinks as pay moves into excluded premiums and wages fall (C-34). Interest groups lobby (C-26).

**Ambiguous effects:** Total heads at bound firms (C-16: fewer full-timers, more part-timers). Wages at unbound firms (C-36: more workers seek those jobs, but those firms gain customers). The general fund's net position after wages adjust (C-35: charges and ICP savings against the appropriation and income tax). Job mobility of newly covered workers once wages adjust (C-28). Business values of capped, near-threshold and compliant firms (C-38, C-46, C-47).

**Key conditions:** K1 binds for 240,000 full-timers (A-1, 0.9); some firms offer (A-2, 0.75) and some pay (A-4, 0.9); wages are sticky in the short run (A-3, 0.75) but adjust as raises are set (A-6, 0.8), not by the whole charge (A-8, 0.85); the minimum wage binds for some (A-9, 0.85); rescheduling below 30 hours is cheap for some (A-10, 0.75); firms near 50 value staying below (A-14, 0.7). Added premises: ICP pays per claim (A-N1, 0.6); collections exceed the appropriation (A-N2, 0.85); some newly covered workers are near the margin of moving (A-N3, 0.6); some seasonal firms lack qualifying cover (A-N4, 0.75); capped staffing firms supply agency labor below the charge (A-N5, 0.55). Under U1-a seasonal firms near 50 bear the cost and cut seasonal hours or contract out; under U1-b nothing changes for them (C-48 to C-50). For the marginal pair at players, the compelled cover is worth less to the worker than its cost (A-7, 0.6), so at least that party is worse off once wages adjust (CR-5b).

**What this analysis cannot say:** How many firms play or pay, how far wages fall, or how many full-time jobs are lost or gained. It does not weigh the cover gained against the wages, hours and jobs lost, or say whether the Act is good. Health effects of cover and of care used are out of scope.

## Gaps found

**(a) Steps no core rule covers.**
- GAP-036-C1: None of the derivations needed a step outside CR-1 to CR-12. The closest was the choice between two ways of complying (offer or pay) whose costs differ by firm and by take-up; CR-4 handles it as a pair of options, but whether the cheaper branch for a firm is "offer" depends on take-up, which no rule addresses (it is the A-2 and A-4 premises).

**(b) Core-rule wording that was hard to apply.**
- CR-1 (0.7, op 3 versus op 4): a capped per-head charge is op 3 for a firm below 100 uncovered and op 4 above it. The clarification defines the operations by whether a choice avoids the charge, which is right, but does not say that the same provision changes operation at a threshold within one firm's choice set (a capped firm that fell below 100 would face op 3 again). Recorded by splitting the class (A-12).
- CR-4 (0.8, cost fixed per firm): the K3 threshold is measured on a past year's average, so the cost attaches to an option exercised a year earlier (hiring in 2027). The rule and CR-10a together covered it, but neither mentions lagged measurement.
- CR-6 with two rules: the minimum wage holds the money wage while the mandate adds to the employer's cost. CR-6 says pressure moves to open margins, but the rule does not say how to treat a mandated term that is itself one of the terms (it is not an open margin, being fixed by K1). Treated the other terms and employment as the margins (C-10).
- CR-7b gross and net: for players the burden borne by workers is gross (they receive cover); A-7 bears on the net, but no variable records a net burden in the worker's own ranking. Recorded gross (C-19) and the CR-5b point only in prose.
- CR-12 0.8 (two items in one variable): applied to employment of full-time and part-time heads (C-14, C-15, C-16). Worked, but the rule does not say whether the total is reported when one item is a reclassification of the other (the same job moved under 30 hours).

**(c) Vocabulary missing from the ontology.**
- No instrument type for a mandate with an alternative charge ("play or pay"); recorded as `mandated_contract_term` plus a per-head charge.
- No variable for take-up of an offered term (how many workers accept a plan), which decides a firm's cost of offering.
- No variable for the size distribution of firms or the number of firms just below a threshold; employment of near-threshold firms (C-22) was used instead.
- No actor class for employers split by compliance branch (players, payers, capped); added as subclasses.
- No variable for the share of jobs below an hours line; `reclassification` and `employment (positions under 30 hours)` were used.

## Comparison with the pattern-based analysis

**Summary.** The two versions agree on the main structure of the play-or-pay case. Both split bound employers by response (offer or pay) and treat capped firms apart. Both find: cover gained at players and better plans at firms with short plans; charges paid to the general fund; employers bearing the cost while wages are sticky; slower wage growth above the minimum as raises are set; workers bearing more of the gross burden over time; full-time jobs cut at the minimum wage, or other terms instead; jobs rescheduled under 30 hours; more hours from remaining full-timers and fewer full-time heads; hiring held back below 50 FTE from the 2027 measurement year; contracting out; misreporting; lobbying; more group plans sold and fewer individual plans; more care billed to insurers and lower ICP outlay; a smaller income tax base; lost sales to small firms and to Tarnley; lower values for bound firms; and compliance and enforcement costs. The core version usually signs the members of the pattern version's mixed groups one by one (C-11 to C-15). That is valid, because one CR-4 switch moves both members. There are six sign differences. Four come from chains the pattern version left out or left uncombined. The capped firms' employment is `no_change` (C-13) against its own C-32. The capped firms' value is a decrease (C-27) against the same C-32. The compliant firms' value is an increase (C-28) with their reporting cost left out. The value of firms at 40 to 49 FTE is a decrease (C-17) against its own C-28; there RS-17's capitalization clause, applied as written, gives the decrease. One comes from a missed arithmetic floor: the pattern version left the general fund's short-run total `ambiguous`, though the capped firms alone owe $13.5 million against $4.1 million. One is a choice of variable: growth past 50 after 2028 is `ambiguous` as a flow and a decrease as a stock. The pattern version omitted several derivable responses, among them hiring through capped staffing agencies (core C-24), job lock while wages are fixed (C-27), workers moving to alternatives (C-21) and the K2 certification cost (C-9). The core version omitted the U1-b shift toward seasonal engagements, players' loss of means, the labor cost per head and total compensation at players. It also overclaims twice: the short-run share borne by employers (C-18), and the loss of means for workers at payers (C-20).

### Findings

- **Pattern overclaims or hidden premises.** RS-17's capitalization clause signs the value of firms near a size threshold as a decrease. It never asks whether the threshold also burdens their larger rivals. Here it does, so for firms below the line the value is `ambiguous` (C-17). RS-18, applied as written to a cap per firm, gives an hours-for-heads shift that does not occur. The core version confirms the pattern version's own finding. The pattern version's replacement, `no_change` for the capped firms' employment, is itself an uncombined chain: the capped firms gain sales and hire more (core C-42). RS-18's Then clause gives "a mixed group" where one CR-4 switch moves both members. That is an underclaim, not an error (C-11, C-12). Hidden premises in the pattern version: that seasonal firms lack qualifying cover (C-19; core A-N4), that the ICP pays per claim (C-36; core A-N1), and that cover is worth less than it costs at players for C-31 (A-7, not listed).

The core claims no short-run income tax loss in C-33, against its own short-run cuts in hours and positions. Its U1-b `no_change` for seasonal firms (C-49) misses the switch to uncounted seasonal engagements. Omissions: players' loss of means (pattern C-43), labor cost per head (C-45), total compensation at players (C-44), the U1-b seasonal substitution by firms near 50 (C-20), and the ICP's administration in the enforcement baseline.
