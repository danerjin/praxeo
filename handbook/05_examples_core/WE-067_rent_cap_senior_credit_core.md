## 1. Policy record

**Tessford Tenant Stability Ordinance 2027 (Ordinance 27-14).** Tessford (about 310,000 people; 128,000 households, 81,000 renting) lies in Ambry County (about 640,000 people). The rest of the county is Corvell (12 km, about 14,000 rental homes), Wick Hollow (25 km) and unincorporated areas: about 41,000 rental homes outside Tessford, vacancy about 4%. Durnside (Selcombe County, about 11,000 rental homes, no rent regulation) is 18 km east, 22 minutes by rail. No rent regulation exists anywhere in the region today. Tessford market rents rose about 6% a year in 2021 to 2026 against about 3% for the price index. Covered buildings hold 52,000 rental homes (vacancy 3%, median rent about $2,000); 55% of covered tenancies are month to month. About 18% of covered households move each year (about 9,400), 38% of movers leave Tessford (about 3,550: 60% to the rest of the county, 40% beyond); 31% of those who left gave a rent rise as the main reason. 7,800 covered households have a member aged 65 or over (6,400 with at least three years' tenure); they move at about 6% a year (about 470), 45% leaving Tessford (about 115 to the rest of the county, about 95 beyond it, of whom about 35 to Durnside). Of households displaced by building sales in 2019 to 2024, 42% left Tessford and 17% left the county.

Figures for the arithmetic: cap about 1.8% a year; on a $2,000 rent the gap to a market rising 6% a year is $272 (11%) in year 3, $489 (18%) in year 5 and $1,191 (33%) in year 10. Withdrawals projected at 150 to 400 units a year from the second year (condominium value per unit 30% to 40% above the value per unit of a capped building); about 15% of households in withdrawn buildings are seniors. Senior credit estimated at $0.6 million in 2028 rising to about $9 million by 2032, from a general fund of about $1.1 billion, with no tax named. The board's fee raises about $4.7 million a year.

Existing rules that stay in the counterfactual: state landlord-tenant law (60 days' notice to end a month-to-month tenancy without cause), the state relocation assistance program for building sales, and state condominium conversion law. In the counterfactual an owner may therefore still convert a building to condominiums, ending tenancies by notice; what K3 changes is the charge and notice attached to doing so, and what K1 changes is the rental yield the owner gives up by converting.

Unclear provisions:
- **U1, displaced seniors and the credit.** U1-a: a senior who leaves on a withdrawal notice "vacates" the unit, and the credit is a City payment, not a claim against the owner, so §9(c) does not bar it; displaced qualifying seniors receive both the $12,000 and the credit. U1-b: §9(c) settles all claims "under this Ordinance", §7 included; displaced seniors receive the $12,000 only. Readings get no probability; where a sign depends on U1, one sign per reading is given.

Not analyzed (as in the record): how the board computes the registered market rent (treated through A-1 as a premise on the size of the gap); partial withdrawal of a building. Also not analyzed: whether a care home or assisted-living residence counts as a "rental dwelling" under §7(c).

K1 is a ceiling only on sitting tenancies: the initial rent of every new tenancy, and every rent in exempt units, stays free. CR-6 therefore applies to the pressure on sitting tenancies (the gap between the capped rent and what the unit would let for), which seeks the margins still open: maintenance, buyouts, sublets, and withdrawal. Prices that stay free (initial rents, exempt rents, county and Durnside rents) move by CR-6 in the ordinary way.

Notes on the derivations:
- **The cap (C-1, C-2).** K1 removes the option of agreeing a sitting tenant's rent above the capped path (CR-1 op 1). Without the ordinance the rent would follow the market within one or two lease cycles (A-5); with it, the rent is held 1.8% a year above its base while the market rises (A-1). The range of acceptable terms is cut off at the cap (CR-5c), and the excess demand at the capped rent is the pressure of CR-6. CR-8b: what sitting tenants do not pay, landlords do not receive.
- **Base-date raises (C-9 to C-12, C-44).** The base is the rent in force on 1 October 2027 (§3). A raise before that date lifts every later capped rent, so landlords who would have raised later raise before (CR-10a; CR-4 with x: raise later, y: raise before), as far as leases renew in the window (A-4). The raise is bounded by the market rent on the base date (CR-5c), and the counterfactual rent passes it within one or two cycles (A-5). In the window sitting tenants pay more (C-9) and have less to spend elsewhere (C-44: they keep renting, CR-3 note). In the first year after the effective date two chains meet: the earlier raise (+) and the cap (−); no premise says which dominates (C-11, C-12). From the medium horizon the cap dominates by arithmetic on the case's figures under A-5: the counterfactual has passed the lifted base and the gap grows each year (C-1).
- **No-cause terminations (C-13 to C-16).** Ending a month-to-month tenancy without cause is open until 1 October 2027 and closed after (K2). A landlord who would have ended a tenancy later ends it before the date, to re-let at the market before the base date or to empty units for withdrawal (CR-10a; A-8). Per window: terminations rise before the date (C-13) and fall after (C-15). Some terminated households' next-best home is outside Tessford (A-14; C-14). Units emptied for withdrawal are held vacant in the window (C-16, with A-12).
- **Holding units empty before withdrawal (C-17).** In a building an owner plans to withdraw, re-letting a vacated unit at the free initial rent adds a household owed $8,000 or $12,000 and 120 days' or a year's notice (K3); holding it empty avoids both (CR-4, x: re-let, y: hold empty). That this pays for some owners is a premise (A-N7).
- **Keepers (C-18 to C-21).** Every move out of a covered unit forfeits the gap, which grows each year (A-1), and cannot be sold by assignment or sublet at a profit (A-3); so staying is made better relative to every move (CR-4b; CR-4a: no keeper moves because of it). The gap is also a gain in means, which could make a move affordable (CR-4 exception); A-27 says the forfeit dominates. The no-cause rule (C-15) removes landlord-initiated moves too. Opposing: buyouts (C-22) move some tenants out for a payment; A-N3 says they move fewer keepers than the cap and the rule hold in place (CR-12e, empirical premise). Moves out of Tessford and out of the county fall with them (C-19, C-20; A-6: some keepers would have left in the counterfactual). Holding a capped tenancy is tied to the place, so jobs that require moving are turned down (relation: complement, location; A-25; C-21).
- **Pressure on sitting tenancies (C-22 to C-27).** The gap between the capped rent and what the unit would let for seeks the margins still open (CR-6): non-price terms (maintenance, C-24 and C-25, given room to cut above the code minimum, A-10, with the tenants' alternative worse because moving forfeits the gap); exchanges on terms the rule does not regulate (buyouts, C-22, A-N2: the landlord pays the tenant part of the gap to vacate, and the unit is re-let at the free initial rent); exchanges outside the rule (sublets above the tenant's rent, C-23, CR-4 with x: comply, y: violate, A-11); and the exit the ordinance itself creates (withdrawal, C-27, CR-6 1.1). CR-6 alone licenses the group (C-26); each member is signed with its own premise.
- **Withdrawal (C-27 to C-30).** K1 lowers the yield of letting a covered building, while the yield of selling its units as condominiums is unchanged; K3 attaches a charge to withdrawing. Owners for whom the condominium value now exceeds the capped rental value plus the charge withdraw (CR-4, x: keep renting, y: withdraw; CR-7a; A-12; A-23: buyers expect the ordinance to last, so the capped value stays low). The counterfactual also allows conversion under state law; the projection of 150 to 400 units a year is against it. Households in withdrawn buildings must move (C-28), and some next-best homes are outside Tessford or the county (A-14; C-29). Displaced seniors: under U1-a they receive the credit, which makes a county home better than leaving (A-7), against the displacement that forces a move (A-14): two opposing chains, no dominance premise, `ambiguous`; under U1-b only the displacement chain applies, `increase` (C-30).
- **Qualifying seniors (C-31 to C-33).** Staying is made better by the gap (A-1, A-2); vacating to a rental home anywhere in Ambry County is made better by the credit, which is the same gap recomputed yearly; leaving the county, living with family or buying brings neither (CR-1 1.1 mirrored: a payment kept only by staying in the jurisdiction is op 3 on leaving). So leaving the county is made worse relative to both alternatives, and some seniors who would have left choose a county option instead (CR-4b; A-6, A-7; CR-4a: none leave because of it): C-31. Out of Tessford (C-32): those who switch from leaving the county to staying in their unit or moving within Tessford reduce it (−); but the credit is paid equally in and outside Tessford, and if the policy raises Tessford rents more than county rents (A-N1, from C-48 against C-57), seniors who would have moved within Tessford move to the rest of the county instead (+): a kind (2) chain with p 0.6, no dominance premise, `ambiguous`. Turnover (C-33): seniors who switch from leaving the county to staying reduce it (−); but between staying and moving within the county, both options gain about the same amount, and the gain in means (the gap, kept either way) can push toward moving to a better home (CR-4 exception), a chain whose own sign is open (kind 3): `ambiguous`.
- **Timing of senior moves (C-34, C-35).** The credit is attached to vacating only on or after 1 October 2027 and after three years' tenure at 65 or over: a dated operation (CR-1 0.7). Seniors who know the conditions (A-9) and would have moved shortly before the date wait until after it (CR-10a; CR-4 with x: move now, y: move after qualifying). Per window: fewer moves before, more just after.
- **Tenure choice of credit holders (C-36).** §7(c) pays only to the landlord of a rental dwelling. A qualifying senior who would have moved in with family or bought a home in the county keeps the credit only by renting (CR-4, x: family home or purchase, y: rent; A-N6).
- **Lobbying (C-37, C-38).** The question of rent regulation was contested on the counterfactual path (A-19: the election, the failed 2022 petition), so lobbying on the field is signed against that contest: both sides would have lobbied anyway, and the direction of the change is not established (CR-4 1.0): `ambiguous`. Lobbying over provisions that exist only under the ordinance (how U1 is read, the board's market-rent schedule, which sets the credit, and the 2032 review) is an increase (CR-4 1.1), given that the groups can organize (A-18); its resources are used up (CR-8c).
- **Asset values (C-39).** The lower expected rent and the charges on exit lower the value of covered buildings (CR-10c), and the loss is borne by whoever owns them when the ordinance becomes expected: partly from the November 2026 election, more on introduction and adoption, as the probability rose (CR-7c; A-23).
- **Administration (C-40 to C-42) and transfers (C-43, C-45).** Registration, complaints and the market-rent schedule use up resources (CR-8c). The County's administration of the credit uses up resources, and the City's 4% fee is a receipt to the County (CR-8b). The rent sitting tenants do not pay is means kept by them and lost by landlords (CR-8b), who also pay the fee, an amount they avoid only by withdrawing (CR-3 note).

Notes on the derivations:
- **Initial rents (C-46).** On a new tenancy the landlord sets the initial rent, but every later rent in that tenancy is capped. The landlord's lowest acceptable initial rent rises by the value of the later increases given up, and the seeker's highest acceptable initial rent rises by the value to them of later rents held down (CR-5c; CR-10c: the tenancy is a durable claim valued by its expected yields). Both ends of the range move up. Fewer vacancies (C-47) also raise the free initial rent toward a higher clearing level (CR-6; CR-6 0.8: a free price's rise suffices).
- **Covered vacancies (C-47).** Two independent chains each lower the number of covered units offered to seekers: keepers stay (C-18; premises A-1, A-2, A-3, A-27, A-N3) and withdrawn units leave the rental stock (C-27; A-12) (CR-12d). Opposing: the turnover of qualifying seniors is a kind (3) chain (C-33). A-N9 says any rise in senior vacancies is smaller than the fall from the other two (CR-12e, empirical premise), so `decrease`.
- **Exempt Tessford rents (C-48 to C-50).** A keeper who stays when they would have moved within Tessford removes a vacancy and a search at once; one who stays when they would have left Tessford (A-6) removes a vacancy and no search, so seekers who would have taken it turn to exempt units, close substitutes (A-22). A withdrawn unit leaves the rental stock; 58% of its displaced households stay in Tessford, and the buyers of converted units vacate a Tessford rental only in part (A-N8), so withdrawal adds to excess demand for the remaining rentals. Both chains raise exempt rents in the medium horizon (CR-9, CR-6; CR-12d, either chain suffices). New building responds only with a lag (A-13), so it is not a medium-horizon opposing chain; for the long horizon no sign is given for exempt rents (no premise on cost per unit as building expands; CR-12 1.0). Exempt owners capture the higher expected rents as the ordinance becomes expected, as far as they expect the exemption to last (CR-7c, CR-10c; A-24; C-49). Developers enter (CR-7a; C-50). The opposing chain, an expected later extension of the cap to newer buildings, needs the negation of A-24 (p 0.4): kind (2) below 0.5, reported in section 10.
- **Rental stock (C-51, C-52).** Withdrawal lowers the Tessford rental stock (C-27), and new building adds to it only with a lag (CR-10b; A-13): `decrease` in the medium horizon. In the long horizon the two chains oppose with no dominance premise (C-52).
- **Condominiums (C-53, C-54).** Withdrawn units are sold as condominiums (or occupied), so more condominiums are sold (C-53). Their price: more supply pushes it down (CR-6 0.9, with the supply in the case's projection); demand from displaced households and shut-out seekers who buy instead of renting pushes it up (A-N4, p 0.55): kind (2), `ambiguous`.
- **Shut-out seekers (C-55, C-56).** Seekers who do not find a covered vacancy at a rent they accept, and face higher exempt rents, settle elsewhere in the county or in Durnside (CR-4; A-15) and commute (relation: complement, location; C-56).
- **County rents (C-57 to C-59).** Pushing up: shut-out seekers (A-15), displaced households who leave Tessford (A-14), and seniors who choose a county home instead of leaving the county (A-7, A-16). Pushing down: fewer keepers and seniors leaving Tessford for the rest of the county (C-19, C-31). A-20 states that the settlers exceed the reduction and that county supply cannot expand at unchanged cost in the medium horizon, which makes the response partial (CR-12e, CR-12 0.9): `increase`. CR-8b: county landlords receive what county tenants pay (C-58); the expected rise is capitalized (CR-7c; C-59).
- **Credit holders' net rent (C-60).** The credit lowers what the holder pays; the policy's rise in county rents raises it. A-17 (the rise is far smaller than the credit) is arithmetic on the case's figures (credit 11% to 33% of rent from year 3; added demand of hundreds of households against 41,000 homes) used as the dominance premise (CR-12e).
- **Durnside (C-61, C-62).** Pushing up: shut-out seekers settling in Durnside (A-15). Pushing down: seniors who would have moved to Durnside now choose a county home (at most about 35 a year, A-7). A-21 states more than about 35 settle there and supply cannot expand at once (CR-12e, CR-12 0.9): `increase`.
- **Who keeps the credit (C-63).** The City pays the credit to the landlord up to the dwelling's rent (§7(c)). Where a holder's credit is at or above the rent of the home they choose, the holder pays nothing at the margin and the landlord's rent to them is drawn toward the credit (CR-6 1.1, third-party payer; A-N5). Where the credit is below the rent, the holder pays every added dollar and no such pull arises.
- **Payers (C-64).** The credit and the 4% fee come from the general fund, with no tax named: someone pays, but CR-8b does not say who; the payers are recorded as a group (CR-8 0.7). Money earmarked for the credit is not available for other City spending (CR-8d).
- **Displaced households (C-65 to C-67).** The relocation payment is a receipt to them and an outlay of the owner (CR-8b; C-7, C-65). Searching and moving use up their means (CR-8c; C-66). Their total: items (+) the capped rent before the notice and the payment; items (−) the moving costs and the policy's higher rents where they move (C-48, C-57): no premise settles it (C-67).
- **Board fund (C-68).** Fees and fines go into the budget that funds enforcement: two items and a net that the case's figures do not settle (CR-8 1.0).

## 8. Time structure

- **Announcement (November 2026 to 30 September 2027).** The ordinance was expected from the November 2026 election, more so on introduction (1 March 2027) and adoption (11 May 2027). Because the base is the rent in force on 1 October 2027, landlords raise rents at renewals before that date (C-9, C-10, C-44) and end month-to-month tenancies without cause while they still can (C-13, C-14, C-16). Seniors who would have moved shortly before the date wait (C-34, C-35). Building values fall, and exempt building values rise, as the measure becomes expected (CR-7c; C-39, C-49). Each of these is signed per window against the counterfactual.
- **The cap tightens each year.** The gap is about 11% by year 3, 18% by year 5 and 33% by year 10 (A-1). Everything that runs on the gap grows with it: keepers' lock-in (C-18 to C-21), the pressure on maintenance, buyouts and sublets (C-22 to C-26), withdrawals (C-27), the credit and its outlay (C-3, C-4, C-63), and the initial-rent premium (C-46). The first year is the only one in which the base-date raise can outweigh the cap (C-11).
- **Withdrawals start in the second year.** Notice is 120 days, or one year where a senior lives in the building, and the projection runs from the second year. Displacement (C-28 to C-30) and holding units empty before withdrawal (C-17) begin then. A withdrawn unit may not be re-let for five years; owners sell or occupy, so the stock change persists (CR-10b; A-13).
- **The credit grows.** Each year adds a cohort of credit holders and each anniversary recomputes their credit on a larger gap: $0.6 million in 2028 to about $9 million by 2032 (C-4, C-64). The capture of the credit by landlords (C-63) is signed only in the long horizon, when credits reach the rents of some homes (A-N5).
- **Durable stock.** Buildings last decades (A-13): new exempt building answers higher exempt rents only over years (C-50), so the rental stock falls in the medium horizon and is unsigned in the long (C-51, C-52).
- **Review in 2032.** A five-year review with no expiry keeps the expectation of long duration (A-23); lobbying over the review is an increase (C-37).
- **Price signals (CR-11a).** Capped rents stop telling sitting tenants what their units are worth to others; keepers act as if their homes were cheaper to hold than they are, which is the lock-in recorded in C-18 to C-21. For qualifying seniors the credit restores part of that signal for moves within the county, since moving no longer forfeits the gap there.

## 11. Report

**The policy:** From 1 October 2027 the rent of a sitting tenant in a Tessford building first occupied before 1990 may rise by at most about 1.8% a year from the rent in force on that date; a new tenancy starts at a rent the landlord sets. Tenants may be evicted only for cause, may not assign their tenancy, and may sublet only at their own rent. An owner may take a whole building off the rental market on 120 days' notice (a year for a senior), paying each household $8,000 ($12,000 for a senior), and may not re-let it for five years. A tenant aged 65 or over who leaves a covered unit after three years receives, for as long as they rent anywhere in Ambry County, a monthly payment to their new landlord equal to the gap between the unit's market rent and its capped rent. Landlords pay $90 a unit a year to a rent board; the credit comes from the City's general fund.

**Visible consequences:** Sitting tenants in covered units pay less from the medium horizon on (C-1, C-43), and their landlords receive less (C-2, C-45). Seniors who move within the county receive the credit (C-3), paid from the City's budget (C-4); landlords pay the board's fee (C-5, C-6). Households in withdrawn buildings receive relocation payments (C-7) and must move (C-28).

**Invisible consequences:**
- *Before the start date:* landlords raise rents at renewal to lift the base (C-9, C-10, C-44) and end month-to-month tenancies without cause while they still can (C-13), some of whose households leave Tessford (C-14); units are emptied for withdrawal (C-16). Seniors near qualifying wait to move (C-34, C-35). Values of covered buildings fall and of exempt buildings rise as the ordinance becomes expected (C-39, C-49).
- *Sitting tenants who are not seniors with three years:* they move less (C-18), leave Tessford and the county less (C-19, C-20) and change jobs less where that means moving (C-21). Some are bought out (C-22); some sublet above their rent (C-23). Maintenance falls and the quality of their homes with it (C-24, C-25). No-cause terminations stop (C-15).
- *Qualifying seniors:* fewer leave Ambry County (C-31). Some rent instead of moving to family or buying, to keep the credit (C-36). Whether they leave Tessford or move house more or less is not established (C-32, C-33).
- *Owners and the stock:* some withdraw buildings and sell the units as condominiums (C-27, C-53), holding units empty first (C-17) and paying relocation (C-65); the Tessford rental stock falls in the medium horizon (C-51).
- *Seekers:* initial rents in covered units are higher (C-46), fewer covered units come vacant (C-47), and exempt rents rise (C-48). Some seekers settle elsewhere in the county or in Durnside and commute (C-55, C-56). Developers build more exempt rental buildings (C-50).
- *Elsewhere:* rents rise in the rest of Ambry County and in Durnside (C-57, C-61), with gains to landlords there (C-58, C-59, C-62); credit holders still pay less net of the credit (C-60), and in the long run some of their landlords capture part of the credit (C-63).
- *Payers and administrators:* taxpayers or other City spending bear the credit (C-64); the board and the County use resources to run the scheme (C-40, C-41), and the County receives its fee (C-42). Interest groups lobby over the provisions that exist only under the ordinance (C-37). Displaced households spend on searching and moving (C-66).

**Ambiguous effects:**
- Sitting tenants' rent in the first year (C-11, C-12): base-date raises against the cap.
- Turnover of all non-qualifying covered households (C-69) and their moves out of the county (C-71): keepers move less, displaced households more. Their moves out of Tessford fall (C-70) only because A-26 says the first exceeds the second.
- Qualifying seniors' moves out of Tessford (C-32) and out of their homes (C-33); displaced seniors' moves out of the county under U1-a (C-30), and all covered seniors' moves out of the county (C-72): the cap and the credit hold some seniors in place, while displacement moves others; whether displaced seniors receive the credit (U1) decides one of these chains.
- Condominium prices (C-54), the long-run rental stock (C-52), displaced households' means (C-67), the board's fund (C-68), and lobbying over rent regulation as a field (C-38).

**Key conditions:** The cap binds and the gap grows (A-1, 0.9) and is enforced (A-2, 0.8); the counterfactual rent follows the market within one or two cycles (A-5, 0.7); withdrawal is used (A-12, 0.75); the credit keeps some seniors in the county (A-7, 0.7); the forfeit of the gap outweighs the savings it builds (A-27, 0.8); keepers' fewer moves out of Tessford exceed displaced leavers (A-26, 0.7); shut-out seekers outnumber the reduction in leavers in the county and Durnside (A-20, A-21, 0.7). Nine premises were added (A-N1 to A-N9), among them buyouts (A-N2, 0.6) and their dominance (A-N3, 0.7), the buyers of converted units (A-N8, 0.6) and the small weight of senior vacancies (A-N9, 0.8).

**What this analysis cannot say:** How large any effect is; whether the ordinance meets its aim of keeping "long-standing residents ... in their community" in total, since several of the relevant moves are ambiguous and the value of community ties is not signed; whether the gains to sitting tenants and credit holders outweigh the losses to owners, seekers, county and Durnside renters and taxpayers; or whether the policy is good. Health and safety effects of lower maintenance are out of scope.

## Gaps found

**(a) Steps no core rule covers.**
- GAP-067-C1. *Flow accounting in a market where buyers are also sellers.* A sitting tenant who does not move removes a vacancy and a search at the same time; whether the net effect on the remaining market is pressure up or none depends on where the stayer would have gone (out of the city, within it). Likewise a withdrawn unit removes supply, while its displaced household and its buyer each may or may not remove demand. CR-6 and CR-9 give the direction of each flow, but no core rule says how to compose the two sides of one household's non-move; the step was done with case-specific counting premises (A-6, A-26, A-N8, A-N9). The same counting sits behind A-20 and A-21.
- GAP-067-C2. *Two options improved by about the same amount.* For a qualifying senior the credit nearly reproduces, for a move within the county, the gain that staying keeps. CR-4 says what happens when x worsens relative to y; it is silent when x and y both improve by amounts that are close but not equal by construction (the credit is computed from the board's schedule and capped at the new rent). C-33 was recorded as `ambiguous` (kind 3, through the CR-4 exception); whether CR-12f's "cancel by construction" should extend to near-cancellation is open.

**(b) Core-rule wording that was hard to apply.**
- CR-1 1.1 states the case of a transfer "avoided only by leaving the jurisdiction" for a charge; the mirror for a grant kept only by staying (the credit ends on leaving the county) follows from the symmetry test but is not written. It was applied mirrored (C-31, C-36).
- CR-6 1.1 (third-party payer) covers a payer who pays "the seller's own posted price up to a limit". The credit is a fixed sum computed from another unit and capped at the new rent; the pull toward the limit exists only for holders whose credit reaches the rent. The clause had to be split by holder (A-N5).
- CR-12 0.8 kind (3) against a dominance premise: for covered vacancies (C-47) the opposing chain (senior turnover) is itself ambiguous, and A-N9 had to cover it in both directions; the rule says this is required but gives no guidance on stating such a premise when the chain's own size is unknown.
- CR-7c "becomes expected" with a staged political path (election, introduction, adoption, effective date) was applied as a probability rising over the window; the rule does not say how to date a short-horizon sign that is spread over ten months.
- CR-10a and the per-window rule (CR-12h) for a qualifying date that differs per household (the third anniversary, the 65th birthday): the windows are household-specific, so C-34 and C-35 are signed "before" and "after the qualifying date" rather than for calendar windows.

**(c) Vocabulary missing from the ontology.**
- `relocation_out_of_area` assumes one area the policy covers; here the cap covers the city and the credit the county, so the area had to be named in a qualifier each time (out of Tessford, out of Ambry County).
- No variable for housing consumption per household (floor area or rooms held relative to household size), which is where the lost price signal of CR-11a shows for sitting tenants and which the credit partly restores for seniors; it was described in section 8 without a consequence.
- No instrument type for a payment that is attached to vacating, portable within an area, sized by another asset's gap and ended by leaving; `lump_sum_subsidy` was used as the closest.
- No generic actor class for households displaced by an owner's lawful exit (`tenants.withdrawn_buildings` was coined), nor for organized interest groups that are neither buyers nor sellers (`organizations` was coined).
- No variable for tenure choice (renting against owning or living with family); C-36 used `quantity_demanded` with a qualifier.

## Comparison with the pattern-based analysis

**Summary.** The two versions agree on the main structure of the Tessford case. Sitting tenants pay less from the medium horizon and their landlords receive less. Base-date raises make the first months `ambiguous`. Non-qualifying keepers move less, leave Tessford less and change jobs less, all on the same premise about the CR-4 exception (A-27). Qualifying seniors leave Ambry County less, while their moves out of Tessford and out of their homes are `ambiguous`. Maintenance and quality fall, and sublets rise. Withdrawals shrink the covered stock and displace households, who leave Tessford more, with the displaced seniors' signs given per reading of U1. The non-qualifying class total out of Tessford is a decrease in the medium horizon on A-26. Exempt, county and Durnside rents rise, and asset values move with them. Lobbying, the credit's payers and the administration costs are recorded on both sides. Both versions also found that PC-21's Unless clause cannot handle a benefit portable over part of the region. There are two sign differences, and in both the pattern version is right. The core version extends A-26, which is a year-3 premise, to the long horizon (C-70). It also signs qualifying seniors' moves in the announcement window `decrease` without combining them with the no-cause terminations (C-34 against C-13). The pattern version's main error is PC-1 and PC-2 applied to seekers. The cap covers sitting tenancies only and the initial rent is free, so vacancies clear at a higher initial rent (core C-46), not through excess demand and rationing. The pattern version also omitted buyouts (C-22). Buyouts are a margin of the CR-6 pressure, and they are an opposing chain that its turnover and relocation decreases need a premise against (A-N3). It also omitted the end of no-cause terminations after the effective date, the landlords' side of the window, initial rents, tenure choice under the credit, holding units empty, and several transfer items and totals. The core version omitted displaced seniors' moves out of Tessford, `quality_adjusted_price`, and A-27 on the seniors' county decrease. Both versions miss one chain. For insiders who still move, the policy raises Tessford's free rents more than county rents (the core's own A-N1), which pushes some of those moves out of Tessford.

### Findings

- **Pattern overclaims or hidden premises.**
  - *PC-1 and PC-2 under vacancy decontrol (analyst misapplication).* The cap binds on sitting tenancies, not on the price seekers pay. Excess demand, rationing and side payments by seekers (C-6, C-7) do not follow, and the initial rent rises instead (core C-46). PC-1's If excludes this case as written, but a note on vacancy decontrol would prevent the error.
  - *PC-7 and PC-21, "cannot be sold".* The benefit cannot be assigned, but it can be surrendered to the landlord for a payment (buyouts, core C-22). With that open, the decreases in turnover and relocation need a premise that bargaining costs keep buyouts below the lock-in (core A-N3). Neither pattern names it.
  - *PC-21's Then, for insiders who still move.* The forfeit is the same for a move within the area and a move out of it. But the policy raises the area's free rents (exempt units, initial rents) more than rents at destinations, so some remaining moves are diverted out of the area. This kind (2) chain needs a dominance premise. Both versions missed it for non-seniors (pattern C-9, core C-19), and the core used it only for seniors (C-32).
  - *C-39.* The pattern's lettings decrease leaves out its own kind (3) senior-turnover chain (A-N9 in the core).
- **Errors in the core version.**
  - C-70 extends a year-3 premise to the long horizon (S).
  - C-34 is not combined with the terminations in the window (S).
  - The core omits displaced seniors' moves out of Tessford (pattern C-23) and `quality_adjusted_price` (C-48). C-31 lacks A-27.
  - C-5 counts the board's fee as resources used up rather than as a transfer.
- **Errors in the pattern version.**
  - C-6 and C-7 (above). The buyout margin is missing, and with it the premise its lock-in signs need.
  - The argument against landlords capturing the credit fails where the credit reaches the rent (C-63).
  - Omissions: the end of no-cause terminations (C-15), initial rents (C-46), tenure choice (C-36), holding units empty (C-16, C-17), the landlords' side of the window (C-10, C-12, C-44), keepers out of the county (C-20), several transfer items and totals (C-42, C-58, C-66 to C-68), and subclass totals per area (C-71, C-72).
- **Test of PC-21 against the core derivation.**
  - *If, "needed interpretation":* confirmed. The core had to treat destinations separately as well (C-31 decrease, C-32 `ambiguous`).
  - *Then, "needed interpretation" (two routes are one) and "failed" for seniors:* both confirmed. The core derives C-19 from the forfeit alone, with being priced out as part of the A-6 counterfactual, and C-32 is `ambiguous`. The core adds two further needs. One is the buyout premise (A-N3). The other is the diverted-movers chain above, which neither version applied to non-seniors.
  - *Subclass split, "needed interpretation" (window terminations; a kind 3 subclass makes the total ambiguous):* confirmed (C-14, C-72). The core contradicts the verdict nowhere, but it errs on the long-horizon total, where the pattern version is right. It also shows that the split is needed per area (C-71), and for turnover too (C-69), where PC-7 has none.
  - *Population caveat, "needed interpretation":* not contradicted. The core gives no population sign. It records the shut-out side as seekers' rentals elsewhere (C-55) rather than as residents' moves.
  - *Assumes, "needed interpretation":* confirmed (A-7 per destination, A-27). The core shows a further missing premise, A-N3 on buyouts.
  - *Unless, "failed":* confirmed. Partial portability neither blocks the county decrease (C-31) nor lets the Tessford decrease through (C-32).
- **Proposed rewording of PC-21, checked against the core rules.**
  - *Sorting destinations into a portable range* is supported. It is CR-4 per pair, with CR-1 1.1 mirrored for a grant kept only by staying in the range.
  - *Moves out of the range decrease, while moves out of the area are `ambiguous` absent a premise on the means effect* is supported. It is CR-4's "a change in means alone" within the range, plus CR-12 0.8 kind 3.
  - *A subclass with an `ambiguous` sign makes the total `ambiguous`* is supported, but the wording should add "unless a dominance premise covers both of its directions" (CR-12 0.8).
  - *The Unless clause for full portability* is supported only where the benefit is equal at every destination. A residual difference, such as a credit capped at the new rent or set from a schedule, is CR-4 proper.
  - *The added CR-4 exception premise* is supported. The rewording still lacks the buyout premise and the diverted-movers chain.
  - *Residents shut out leave the area more* is supported with A-15 and A-22. Under vacancy decontrol it runs through higher free rents, not rationing.
- **Limits of the core rules.** None produced a difference.
  - *GAP-067-C1 (one household's non-move removes a vacancy and a search):* not a calculus gap. CR-9 applied per relation gives two chains in the same market: the vacancy not offered is a supply change, and the search not made is a demand change where the stayer would have moved within the city. CR-6 applies in each segment and CR-12b, d and e combine them. The share of stayers who would have left the city is a count over a distribution, and CR-12e 0.8 already says that this is arithmetic where the case gives the distribution (38% of movers leave) and a premise otherwise. At most it is a wording gap: CR-9 could say that one actor's single choice can sit on both sides of one market, and that the two sides are separate chains. The pattern version did not face the step. It counted every lost vacancy as a shut-out seeker (PC-3, PC-8, C-31), which overcounts but gives the same sign. Its evidence for A-20 uses only the leavers' vacancies, which is the core's count.
  - *GAP-067-C2 (CR-4 when two options improve by nearly the same amount):* not a calculus gap. CR-4 is stated over relative cost. Split the change into a common part (the smaller improvement), which is "a change in means alone" for whoever stays within the set, and a residual difference, which is CR-4 proper with the sign of the difference. The common part follows from CR-1 1.1, mirrored by the symmetry test: a transfer kept only by staying is op 4 for those who stay and op 3 on leaving. The residual's sign differs per senior, because the registered market rent can lie above or below the unit's own letting value and the credit is capped at the new rent. That makes it kind 3, and the result is `ambiguous`. CR-12f is not needed, since near-cancellation is not cancellation by construction, and the kind 3 rule already gives the right answer. This is a wording gap: CR-4 could state the decomposition. The pattern version took the same step by reading CR-4 per pair of options, with "a change in means alone" within the county set (its Gaps (b) 1). It reached the same signs (C-11, C-52 against core C-32, C-33) and logged the step as unclear wording, which is the right classification.
  - *Unclear core-rule wording that both versions met:*
    - CR-1 1.1 is written for a charge, not its mirror for a grant.
    - CR-4's "change in means alone" is stated for exact equality across options.
    - CR-4's exception is written for burdens, not for a benefit attached to one option.
    - CR-12 0.8 does not say whether arithmetic can bound a kind 3 chain.
    - CR-6 1.1 does not cover a fixed sum capped at the rent.
    - CR-12h does not cover household-specific qualifying dates.
