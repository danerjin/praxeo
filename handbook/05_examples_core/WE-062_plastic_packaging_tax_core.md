# WE-062 / Analysis: Tax on plastic packaging, with a recycled-content exemption and a small-firm threshold

Analyst: core-only re-run (Handbook 1.0)  |  Rules: core rules CR-1 to CR-12 only  |  Status: awaiting comparison

## 1. Policy record

**Saldria Plastic Packaging Tax Act.** Saldria is a country of about 9.4 million people with its own floating currency, the taler (T). It produces no virgin resin; all is imported at world prices (about 1,150 T a tonne), and Saldria buys about 0.4% of world packaging resin. About 610,000 t of plastic packaging is placed on the market a year: about 330,000 t made by about 210 domestic converters (about 19,000 employees), 120,000 t imported empty and 160,000 t arriving around imported goods. Converters export about 70,000 t of empty packaging, and about 45,000 t leaves around exported goods, at prices set abroad. Average recycled content is about 11%; about 85,000 t (14%) is already at 30% or more; food-contact flexible films (about 150,000 t) are essentially 0% recycled, and the Food Safety Authority approves recycled plastic for food contact only from approved PET processes. Seven domestic plants make about 48,000 t of recycled resin a year from collected plastic; about 12,000 t is imported (Saldria buys about 3% of the regional market). Municipalities collect about 140,000 t a year and auction bales at about 300 T a tonne, near the capacity of routes and sorting lines. Substitutes: two board mills at about 92% of capacity (board also traded regionally), one glass plant at 88% (a new furnace takes about two years), one can maker. About 3,900 firms are at or above 10 t; about 17,000 firms handle under 10 t (about 25,000 t, 4%); about 520 firms are at 7 to 10 t and 380 at 10 to 13 t. Compliance costs 2,500 to 4,000 T a year per registered firm. Packaging is 1% to 3% of the price of most packaged goods and 6% to 10% for bottled water and pre-packed produce. Treasury's static projection is 98 million T a year.

Existing rules, unchanged on both paths: the Food Safety Authority's rule on recycled plastic in food contact, customs procedures, the general sales tax, municipal collection contracts. Counterfactual law: no charge on plastic packaging; a ban campaign continues on both paths.

Unclear provisions:
- **U1, how the 30% is measured.** U1-a (per component): 4(2) fixes only the measurement period; each kind of component must itself reach 30%. U1-b (averaged): if the person's average over all its components in the quarter is 30% or more, every component it made or imported that quarter is exempt; otherwise none is. No probability is given to either reading.

## 8. Time structure

- **Announcement (2 March to 31 December 2027).** The charge was expected ten months before it took effect. Liable firms bring orders and manufacture forward and store packaging (CR-10a; C-21, C-22, C-24); the next quarters' purchases are lower (C-23). Near-line firms' 2027 tonnage already decides their 2028 liability under K3, so their response begins at the announcement, not on 1 January 2028 (C-14). Asset values of recycling plants rise and those of export-oriented and film converting plants fall part of the way at announcement and the rest at enactment on 28 June (CR-7c, probability rising; C-35, C-52, C-53).
- **First year.** Board and glass capacity is fixed (A-7), so substitute prices rise and other buyers are crowded out (C-41, C-44, C-45). Recycled resin supply expands only at a rising cost (A-10) and collection is near capacity, so bale and resin prices rise (C-37, C-32).
- **Medium run.** Converters add equipment for recycled resin (C-31); recyclers enter (C-36); municipalities extend collection (C-38); board and glass capacity is added (C-42); intermediaries certify content (C-30).
- **Long run.** Export converting and filling lines are replaced abroad as they wear out (CR-10b; C-29). The rate is fixed in talers and not indexed, so as other prices rise the charge falls in real terms and binds less each year; no sign is given for that drift.
- **Quarterly averaging under U1-b.** Under U1-b a person can also time its high-content production within a quarter to lift the average; this is a path note, not a separate consequence.
- **Persistence.** Not a repeal; no persistence field is needed.

## 11. Report

**The policy:** From 1 January 2028 Saldria charges 200 T a tonne on plastic packaging components manufactured in or imported into the country, empty or around imported goods, with no relief on export. Components with 30% or more recycled content are exempt; it is unclear whether the 30% is measured per component (U1-a) or as each firm's quarterly average (U1-b). Firms that made or imported under 10 t in the previous year are exempt; a firm over the line pays on all its tonnage. A plastics unit funded with 7 million T a year administers it; the revenue goes to the general budget.

**Visible consequences:** Users of taxed plastic packaging pay more for it (C-1), and buyers of packaged goods, most of all bottled water and pre-packed produce, pay more (C-54). Less taxed plastic is used (C-6), more board, glass and metal (C-9), and the average recycled content of plastic packaging rises (C-7). The budget collects the tax (C-3).

**Invisible consequences:**
- *Converters:* they keep less per tonne after tax (C-5); exports of empty packaging fall and keep less (C-25, C-26); export plants and film plants lose value from the announcement (C-52, C-53); some invest in recycled-resin equipment (C-31); export lines are replaced abroad in the long run (C-29). Single-product film makers bear more than multi-product rivals under U1-b (C-13). Domestic makers of documented recycled-content components gain sales from undocumented imports (C-50).
- *Liable firms:* compliance costs (C-2); redesigns so that plastic is not the heaviest material (C-11); under U1-b, product mixes combined to reach a 30% average (C-12); misdeclaration (C-18) and penalties (C-19); stocking up before 2028 and buying less early in 2028 (C-21, C-22, C-23, C-24).
- *Small firms:* firms near 10 t cut own imports from 2027 and buy from liable suppliers (C-14, C-15); small users import their own packaging (C-16); small importers of packaged goods sell more (C-17).
- *Exporters of packaged goods:* keep less and export less (C-27, C-28).
- *Recycling chain:* recycled resin prices and output rise (C-32, C-33), recyclers hire (C-34), their plants gain value (C-35), entrants come in (C-36); municipalities get more per bale and collect more (C-37, C-38); more recycled resin is imported and regional prices rise slightly (C-39, C-40); certifiers gain work (C-30). Content in components already above 30% falls under U1-a (C-8).
- *Substitutes:* board and glass prices rise in the first year (C-41) and capacity is added later (C-42, C-43); other buyers of board and glass pay more and buy less (C-44, C-45). Buyers move from pre-packed to loose goods (C-55, C-56).
- *Foreign sellers:* less virgin resin is sold to Saldria and its world price falls very slightly (C-46, C-47); foreign converters sell less empty packaging and keep slightly less (C-48, C-49).
- *Budget and others:* the plastics unit costs 7 million T (C-4); the net receipt (C-59) benefits other taxpayers or other spending (C-60); users who keep paying have less for other things (C-58); revenue falls short of the static projection (C-61); groups lobby over U1 (C-20).

**Ambiguous effects:** Employment in converting (C-51): lower tonnage, exports and small users' imports push it down; the shift from undocumented imports to documented domestic components pushes it up. The exchange rate (C-57): fewer imports push the taler up, fewer exports push it down. Recycled content of components already above 30% under U1-b (C-8): dearer resin pushes it down, averaging pushes it up for multi-product firms. What would decide each: the relative sizes of the opposing flows.

**Key conditions:** Collection (A-1, 0.85); users bear more than sellers (A-2, 0.75); users can substitute (A-3, 0.85) and reach 30% cheaply (A-4, 0.85); recycled supply expands only at a rising cost (A-10, 0.7); export prices set abroad (A-12, 0.8); near-line firms can stay under 10 t (A-14, 0.7); stocking before 2028 (A-16, 0.75); misdeclaration pays for some (A-17, 0.6); undocumented imports (A-29, 0.65); the small users' shift is smaller (A-31, 0.65). Two premises were added: redesign so plastic is not the heaviest material (A-N1, 0.65), and loose goods as substitutes (A-N2, 0.8). U1 changes the signs of C-12, C-13 and C-8.

**What this analysis cannot say:** The size of any effect: how much of the charge users bear, how much plastic use falls, how much revenue is raised, whether converting employment or the exchange rate rises or falls. It does not weigh gains to recyclers and substitute makers against losses to users, exporters and converters, or say whether the policy is good. Environmental effects, including litter, emissions and the fate of plastic waste, and health and safety effects (food-contact safety) are out of scope.

## Gaps found

**(a) Steps no core rule covers.**
- GAP-062-C1: K3's previous-year rule makes the base of a dated notch (2027 tonnage) a choice made before the charge exists. CR-1's 1.0 notch note and CR-10a together cover it, but no rule says how to sign a response in a period when the policy is not yet in force but its base is already being measured; it was recorded as a short-horizon consequence from the announcement (C-14).
- GAP-062-C2: the net effect on the recycled-content share needed arithmetic over two quantities (recycled resin up, plastic tonnage down). No rule covers composite ratios; the sign rests on arithmetic plus the response argument, which CR-12e allows, but the 1.0 note speaks only of prices.

**(b) Core-rule wording that was hard to apply.**
- CR-1 per reading: under U1-b the exemption is a notch on an average over a person's whole output, so the operation on one component depends on the person's other components. CR-1 records operations per actor class, not per actor's product mix; the class had to be split by product mix (A-9).
- CR-7b: the incidence premise A-2 compares users with sellers; the rule says a comparative premise is needed for each pair. With converters, foreign converters, users and final buyers there are four parties; only two comparisons were supplied, so the final buyers' share was signed by direction only.
- CR-12 (0.8, two items): domestic converters' output has two items moving in opposite directions (taxed components down, documented exempt components up). The note says to record the total separately; with no premise the total would be ambiguous, but the variable has no natural total in tonnes when one item replaces imports and the other loses to board. Left without a total.
- CR-4 (1.0, lobbying): for the ban campaign, the case supplies only a premise that spending is unchanged (p 0.45). Its negation does not give a direction, so nothing could be reported in either direction.

**(c) Vocabulary missing from the ontology.**
- No variable for the share of a mixed product made of one material (recycled content is `product_attribute`, which also had to carry plastic weight per component). A `material_intensity` variable would separate them.
- No actor class for firms defined by their product mix (single-product against multi-product), which U1-b needs.
- No variable for exports as such: `quantity_supplied (exports)` was used.
